# E5b remainder: dynamic native acquisition, dirty recovery and held-policy execution

Planning record, 9 November 2026. **Docs only; no implementation change.** Base: `origin/codex/persistent-city` at `c2fc1b0` (F5h #163), current writer **34** (`CityState.CompactSchema = ChangeJournalSchema + 1` and `CurrentSchema = CompactSchema`, `Core/Model.cs:339-340`). Proposed types, members, budgets and effort are designs, not existing APIs or measured results.

Roadmap row: "E5b complete-menu integration | partial | Dynamic native acquisition, dirty recovery and held-policy execution are not done." There is no GitHub issue for E5b. The contract is the "Refusal boundary and remaining implementation" section of the [E5b checks](../checks/e5b-complete-menu.md#refusal-boundary-and-remaining-implementation), the E5b row and sections 4, 5 and 8 of the [E5 plan](e5-dynamic-products.md), and what [E6f](e6-carts-food-trucks.md#4-slice-breakdown) needs.

**What the player gets.** Every food product the game actually loads, vanilla or modded, can be sold to residents in your own shops, provided the whole shop meets the same safe contract the original three products meet today. When you rearrange a shop, change stock or menu, or another mod changes things behind the scenes, the city notices before it sells anything on stale terms and hands the shop back to the game's own customers until the next safe hour. Residents' held choices survive save and reload unchanged.

## 3. Decisions

**Decision 1: correctness never depends on notifications.** Harmony postfixes on the 19 E5a members, plus `ParseIntoItemInstance`, `TemporarilyClose`, `RemoveUnusedRetailPrices` and `CargoInstance.ResetItemCached`, only mark a shop dirty sooner. They are coalesced per shop (one queued unit however many calls) and carry no authority. Catalogue postfixes (W11) bump the catalogue revision, which invalidates every shop image. Reason: the inventory above proves the surface cannot be exhaustive, so a missing hint may cost latency but must never cost correctness. No per-instance `OnItemsInCargoUpdated` subscription: it is not invoked on every path either, and it would attach PC delegates to native objects that outlive a city.

**Decision 2: a constant-time structural stamp on every existing discovery visit.** For each registration, read the references and `_version` values of `cachedAvailableProducts`, `retailPrices` and `itemInstances`, their counts, `businessTypeName` by reference, `RentedByPlayer`, `HasEstablishedBusiness`, `AvailableForRent` and `temporarilyClosed`. Compare with the stamp recorded in the published image. A mismatch marks the shop dirty and calls `NativeFoodOwnership.Observe(reg, false)` in the same visit, which stops new captures at once. The E5 plan section 5 asks for exactly this: discovery consumes a prebuilt result "using constant-time revision/dirty checks". Reason: it catches W3–W6 and W8–W10 row changes within one ring revolution at no allocation, without a scan.

**Decision 3: a bounded contract recheck at each selected capture and checkout.** Before a capture is published and again after native setup and before the receipt, compare, for every contract holder in the image: the `ItemInstance` reference under its `itemInstances` key, the `cargoInstances` reference, `_version` and count, the stock `CargoInstance` reference, its `itemName` (ordinal) and empty nested list; every definition the image depends on (Decision 3a); and every price row's name and exact price. Bounds: at most 8 offers and the image's holder list (≤ 128 entries per collection). Reason: this is where W7, W10 and in-place W11 writes would matter, and the E5 plan section 5 places the safety check "at a real decision/sale". Quantities are not compared (Decision 6).

**Decision 3a: definition dependencies are compared by content, and a change rebuilds the catalogue.** `LoadedStockCatalogue` copies definition fields and arrays once per catalogue revision (`Core/LoadedStockEligibility.cs:36`, `Whitelist`/`Outputs` at `:51-52`), so a shop rebuild alone would reuse stale facts. Each published image therefore lists every definition it used: each menu product `Item`, each contract holder's and bag counter's `Item`, the bag `Item`, and the `BusinessType`. For each one it records the live reference and the copied facts the contract read: type flags, seasonal, gift and takeover fields, `cargoCapacity`, `itemsThatCanShowcase`, `itemsWhitelist`, `producerSettings.ResourcesRequired` and `itemsToProduce` **element by element** (ordinal, bounded at 128), authored tag lists, and for the business type its simulator type, `hasEntranceFee` and authored tags. The check compares live content with the copy, not array references, so an in-place element edit is caught. Distinct definitions are compared once per capture batch and shared across the shops in it. Any mismatch withdraws the shop, bumps the catalogue revision and re-copies the affected definitions from the live loaded objects, so every shop image built afterwards uses fresh facts. Decision 4's preparation runs the same comparison for every definition an owned or acquiring shop depends on before rebuilding that shop. This also closes the reconciliation blind spot: native `SetUpAvailableShowcaseShelves` indexes a showcase by its cargo name, so a changed `itemsToProduce` on that showcase passes Decision 5 and is caught only here.

**Decision 4: every ownership boundary decides on an image prepared during the hour before it.** The boundary itself stays synchronous in `AfterNativeTick` (refresh, `lastBoundaryHour`, drain, `FoodAuthority.Boundary`, `Runtime/NativeFoodOwnership.cs:279-320`); the fresh read happens beforehand, across frames:

1. *Prepare.* When accounting hour *h* begins, the coordinator queues every PC-owned shop, every shop whose authority wants acquisition and every known refused or recovering shop (scopes PC has seen, at most 1,024 in total) for a full rebuild (Decision 3a's definition check first, then the E5a shop pass) in bounded units, at most 0.5 ms per frame. 1,024 shops took 10.5 ms in total offline (section 4), about 21 frames, against several hundred frames per game hour even at native speed 6. Each result is stamped with the hour it was built in.
2. *Serve while rebuilding.* A queued or in-progress rebuild does **not** change ownership: `SupportedMenu` keeps answering from the last published image while its Decision 2 stamp still matches, Decision 3 still guards every sale, and `Observe(reg, true)` is passed through unchanged, so `RequestedHour` is not reset. Native demand stays suppressed for hours PC already owns, as today.
3. *Publish.* A passing rebuild replaces the published image atomically, with no ownership change. A failing rebuild, a stamp mismatch or a failed recheck calls `Observe(reg, false)` at once: no new captures, existing receipts and returns kept. The handoff itself happens at the **next** accounting boundary after the ending-hour drain, through the unchanged `FoodAuthority.Boundary` rule (`hour > RequestedHour`).
4. *Commit at boundary h+1.* After `ForceCompleteAllWork`, each candidate transfer is validated again: its Decision 2 stamp must still equal the published image's, and the image must have been built and passed in hour *h* or later, after the shop's `RequestedHour`. An acquiring shop then gets one last synchronous gate before it transfers: Decision 3a's content comparison of its definition dependencies and Decision 3's contract-holder slot check, both run after the drain. These catch in-place edits made after preparation passed, such as an `itemsToProduce` element changed on a holder definition with no capture in between, which move no stamp. The gate costs about one Decision 3 recheck per shop (target ≤ 0.05 ms), distinct definitions are compared once per boundary, and at most 16 acquisitions commit per boundary (≤ 0.8 ms in the boundary frame). Any further acquisitions are deferred to the next boundary. A shop that passes acquires; a shop that fails is refused there and stays native. Owned shops do not need this gate: they make no sale without Decision 3, and the next hour's preparation rebuilds them. A shop that is not ready (dirtied in the hour's last frames, or its rebuild unfinished) is **deferred**: the boundary skips it without touching `Desired` or `RequestedHour`, so it transfers at the first later boundary at which it is ready. An owned shop that is not ready keeps ownership for one more hour under Decisions 2 and 3; if its rebuild then fails, step 3 hands it back.

This needs a readiness input to `FoodAuthority.Boundary` and a deferred state that preserves `RequestedHour`, which today is reset whenever `Desired` flips (`Core/FoodAuthority.cs:35-38`). Unchanged shops therefore keep service through every boundary, invalid shops hand back one boundary after detection, and eligible shops acquire at the first boundary after a passing image. This is the one place this plan departs from the E5 plan's text ("No timer or full-shop fingerprint scan is the fallback", section 5). Reason: ownership changes only at accounting boundaries, so a read completed in the hour before each one makes the acquire/suppress decision exhaustive for every writer in section 2.2, including in-place writes on non-contract furniture. It is not a polling fallback for sales, which Decisions 2 and 3 guard. The cost is bounded by owned shops (≤ 1,024 scopes) and is measured in E5b3 (section 4). The coordinator may record this in `docs/decisions/` after review.

**Decision 5: effective-holder reconciliation after native setup.** Whenever `FoodSupplyReader.ReadMenu` has run `SetUp` (capture, checkout, recovery and boundary), compare per menu product the native `availableProducers` entries with the image's validated holders: every native producer must be an image holder by instance reference, none may carry the `EmployeeWorkstation` flag, and an image holder with positive cargo that native setup omits is unknown, not zero. Native `pointOfSales` counters must be image bag counters. Any mismatch withdraws the whole business. Reason: this closes the gap at `Runtime/FoodSupplyReader.cs:105-118` and is the E5 plan's "Reconcile actual eligible producers with the validated holders". It reuses the existing setup, so it adds no `SetUp` calls.

**Decision 6: quantity is live, never a dirty source.** W1, W2 and W3 amount changes on contract holders do not dirty the image. Units, bags and capacity keep coming from the live supply read at capture and checkout. Known zero (a retained same-ID slot with amount 0) stays zero; a missing or malformed slot stays unverified (existing rule, E5b review round 1). `ReduceFromCargo` removing a slot is a shape change and does dirty (W5).

**Decision 7: dynamic dirty recovery replaces the original-three-only path.** A refused or dirty shop is revalidated only at an advancing accounting hour, after drain, with an image prepared during the hour before that boundary (Decision 4), the price/bag/supply read and Decision 5. Success requests the normal later acquisition boundary; failure, the refused hour itself, undrained work and live native customers keep it native. Refused shops are in Decision 4's preparation queue, so a repair that no hook or stamp sees, such as an in-place definition fix after handback, still gets a fresh image in the next hour. Recovery work is a bounded queue: at most 64 recovery revalidations per frame, each its own continuation unit. Reason: same safety rules as `RevalidateRefusedContract` (`Runtime/NativeFoodMarket.cs:349-370`), generalized from "refused original-three shop" to any contract, with a storm bound.

**Decision 8: capacity preflight before admission.** Admission calls `CompleteFoodMenu.Check` with the shop's actual retained history products and the city's referenced name table, so a ninth history cell or a 257th name refuses before any native effect (`Core/CompleteFoodMenu.cs:28-31`). The `nativeContract` input is set only from Decisions 2–5 passing for that shop, never from config.

**Decision 9: held-policy execution takes one schema bump.** Executing `DynamicWire` either changes the header of every new city (its golden hash moves) or flips a city's policy mid-life, which is a switch-over day. The delivery rules require a bump in both cases. E5b4 takes the next free writer at merge: new cities are created with `MenuPolicy = DynamicWire`, it becomes executable, and cities from older writers are refused with the "start a new city" status and their payload kept. No `NamePolicyStartsDay` activation is used; the field stays on the wire unchanged.

**Decision 10: keep the implemented taste domain.** The executable domain is `pc:product-name-taste:v1|<idLength>:<id>|<nameLength>:<name>` as implemented and pinned by E5c (`Core/FoodProductMetadataResolver.cs:86`; reference SHA-256 `937617912b…949e` in the [E5c checks](../checks/e5c-neutral-metadata.md)). The E5 plan's `pc:product-taste:name:v1` spelling is not adopted. Reason: E5c's golden vectors are already reviewed, and with Decision 9 no saved city holds draws under either domain, so nothing needs rerolling.

**Decision 11: Core executes any canonical, loaded, Food-class name the market supplies.** E5b4 replaces the `FoodProducts.Known` gates in live choice and meal tickets (`Core/FoodWeightedCommerce.cs:198`; `Core/FoodMealTickets.cs:35,66,86`) with a market-supplied `IFoodProductPolicy`: canonical name, present in the published loaded-name image, resolver class Food. Capture writes `resolver.Capture(...)` revision-2 metadata for non-original names and keeps legacy revision-1 capture for the original three (`Core/FoodWeightedCommerce.cs:211`). Weighting uses the existing metadata overload (`Core/FoodChoicePolicy.cs:117-121`). Held tuples replay as saved; config or API changes apply at the next uncaptured context. Rivals stay original-three (`FoodAlternativeObservation`, `RivalFoodCatalogue`): rival offers are observation only and widening them is outside E5b.

**Decision 12: the capability refusal stays until each runtime capability is proven present.** Dynamic acquisition (E5b5) switches on only when all of these pass at city start: the `_version` readers bind with type `int`, a startup self-test on private lists and dictionaries proves increment on add, remove and indexer set; every Decision 1 hook installs; `availableProducers`/`pointOfSales` bind; the E5a member discovery succeeds. If any fails, the original-three guard and `DynamicIntegrationRefusal` remain, and `pc food contract` reports which capability failed. There is no config switch. Native proof is the batched rig run (section 7); under merge rule v2 it does not gate merge, and a failed rig check becomes a fix slice.

**Decision 13: shadow first.** E5b2 and E5b3 run the new stamps, images, rechecks, reconciliation and recovery against today's shops while the original-three guard still decides which products are admitted. Original-three shops get the stricter invalidation immediately. E5b5 then removes the guard. Reason: the machinery is measured and rig-checked on known products before any new product gains authority.

## 8. Open questions

These are reserved product decisions. The plan proceeds on the recommended default.

- **Q1. Shops with five to eight products.** Should residents shop at a shop of yours that sells five to eight products? Recommended default: yes, up to eight; a shop with more than eight products keeps the game's own customers. (E5b5 raises the live limit and the default from 4 to 8.)
- **Q2. Changing a price.** When you change a price at a shop residents are using, should the shop keep its residents? Recommended default: yes. A resident who chose the old price picks again at the counter; changes to products, equipment, staff, bags or opening hours still hand the shop back to the game's customers until the next safe hour. (Today any price change hands the shop back, `Runtime/NativeFoodMarket.cs:519-523`.)
