## Product promise
Persistent residents have homes, workplaces and leisure destinations. Their journeys and cars continue beyond the camera, so looking away and back does not replace the city. Their local food choices make prices, location, opening hours, staffing and shop capacity matter. Big Ambitions continues to run stock, suppliers and business accounts.

The first release delivers this experience for a bounded verified food category. It does not replace every retail category or the entire economy. C# and the existing population/traffic foundation remain. This plan replaces the older whole-city-economy roadmap as the required scope.

## Keep, simplify and leave out
Keep stable identities and appearances; persistent home/work/study/leisure assignments; real walk/subway/car journeys; car ownership, parking location and citywide traffic; sensible street waiting; breakfast/lunch/dinner; croissants, hot dogs and burgers under native equipment rules; finite individual wallets; native checkout and accounting; a concise business feedback panel.

Simplify to one supported runtime mode and one current save format on a new disposable development baseline. Drop historical experimental migrations and temporary allowance modes from the shipped path. Preserve unsupported/unreadable payloads unchanged and report the problem; never silently reset an existing payload. Saves created by the supported version must preserve identities, ongoing journeys, balances and receipts. Automatic game/RLO contract checks remain; they are not user settings.

Residents stay in the city for this milestone. Remove random departure/replacement and its bookkeeping from required behavior. Workplaces can change; people and cars do not need to be replaced to create variation.

Out of scope: native payroll integration, job contracts/career histories, family/shared household accounting, physical groceries/cooking, rival financial strategy or bankruptcy, custom production/import/delivery systems, general itinerary/detour planning, new product categories, detailed parking/boarding/train bodies or interiors, elaborate social scenes, and a Rust rewrite. Existing shadow evidence stays archived as development evidence, never as a second sales engine.

## Consumer representation: two first, three if justified
One physical resident is an anchor for K economic consumers, including the original consumer: K=2 means two total, not two additional. K=1 is the comparison baseline; K=2 is the initial candidate. Test K=3 only if the K=2 findings justify it. K is one developer balance value saved at city initialization. Changing it requires a fresh development comparison, not hot changes to an existing city's money or identities.

Each consumer gets a stable anchor-plus-slot ID, its own once-initialized wallet, declared external income/support and expenses, modest stable product tastes, finite meal intentions and receipt keys. Consumers share the anchor's broad home/work/leisure pattern and coarse employment state. They are represented demand, not additional physical households, occupied homes or cars. Display physical and represented populations separately. Do not multiply a paid sale, a wallet's purchasing power, staff throughput or vehicle counts.

The original consumer retains its real resident journey. Additional consumers can choose independently from an eligible shared routine stop, using a small saved meal-excursion record on the existing bounded scheduler: origin, destination, valid travel, arrival/service/return timing and outcome. They require no independent body, car, general daily itinerary or traffic solver. Shared routine opportunities exclude the anchor's personal food detour: capture their base origin and departure deadline before consumers diverge. An anchor's food detour does not relocate all consumers. Independent food trips remain walking-only in this increment, as in the current food routine; subway belongs to the shared daily travel pattern.

Outbound and return routes must be feasible, and travel, queue, service and eating must fit the shared activity opportunity. No new meal trip begins during a committed routine journey or while that consumer is still away. An interrupted or overrun excursion cannot snap to the anchor's new location or become a successful visit; preserve its unresolved ownership and make it unavailable for new purchases until safely resolved. After a completed return, a consumer can rejoin at a matching shared place/time. If it misses that shared departure, resynchronize the idle abstract consumer at the anchor's next safe home stay. This resynchronization abstracts non-food travel; it never completes an unfinished excursion, resolves an uncertain checkout, grants money, or changes a physical resident/car. Outstanding returns or checkout uncertainty stay isolated and visibly reported, and must not accumulate in ordinary play. No general itinerary-repair subsystem is introduced. Share only route geometry/timing whose endpoints and validity match, never personalized choices, wallets, stock reservations or queue positions. Separate consumer identity from physical-anchor identity at the meal/receipt boundary.

Every completed purchase uses one available service slot, its actual price, one product and the required bag through the native transaction path. Keep existing-stop purchasing for the physical anchor without extending it; additional consumers use their bounded dedicated outings. Every path retains the same consumer/meal identity. Uncertain outcomes retain held funds and receipts and are never blindly retried. Reconcile only against trustworthy native/persistent receipt evidence: confirm a proven sale or refund a proven refusal; neither missing evidence nor elapsed time proves failure. Isolate an unresolved consumer, report it clearly and do not silently write off funds or freeze unrelated consumers. An ordinary operating campaign leaving unresolved purchases is a release blocker. Daily income, costs, native hours, cash and stock reconcile under reload and different event budgets. The representation layer must not grow into a second full citizen simulator; if the small excursion contract cannot be implemented within the existing scheduler, resolve that architecture before expanding scope.

K increases opportunities, not guaranteed customers. A large shop should gain useful throughput when nearby serviceable demand exceeds smaller capacity. A bad location can still fail. Do not increase K indefinitely to force a prescribed profit or sales count.

## Simple job changes and efficient transport
Workers are assigned or seeking. Apply rare daily job-loss chances and separate job-finding chances with stable resident/day randomness, once per elapsed day through bounded processing. Students and retirees retain their own routines. A successful search chooses a reachable suitable workplace within existing modeled assignment capacity; failed searches stay seeking. Keep a small bounded search and retry later, not a citywide labor-market solver.

Changes take effect at a safe routine boundary without canceling an underway commute or moving the car. Seeking workers retain home/leisure activities and lower declared outside support; finding work restores their saved income basis. Update income at the next unposted day, without regranting savings or rewriting past postings. Represented consumers inherit coarse employment timing but retain their own income bases and balances. No employment history or employer-led payroll is needed.

Subway is an abstract timed middle leg between actual station walks. Keep bounded mode choice, native access validation and persistent cars. Reuse validated building/station connectors, station-pair geometry and familiar routes where suitable, with bounded caches and invalidation on relevant changes. Current departures still check live access, car pickup position and applicable traffic costs; committed trips retain continuity. Do not build an unbounded whole-city precompute or recalculate every mode every frame.

Measure actual mode shares, query counts, cache hits, route time and frame pacing. More transit eligibility does not prove savings. A subway-heavy run with no persistent cars cannot validate the central traffic promise. K does not multiply traffic; physical demand and represented food demand are explicitly different scales.

## Plug and play with one business owner
Release UX is install dependencies/mod and load a supported city. No manual shop enrollment, experimental economy selector or gameplay tuning panel. Automatically manage eligible player food businesses; the game keeps entire ownership of unsupported businesses/categories. A small status message explains the modeled scope and any excluded shop. The management panel shows sales/operating result, meal/product timing and actionable lost-demand reasons, including stock versus unstaffed/sick service. Diagnostics stay developer-only.

Design and verify the business ownership/operating boundary at K=1 before increasing represented demand. Own an entire verified business, never just its supported menu items alongside native customer generation. An unsupported/mixed menu remains native; a supported menu edit refreshes its verified offers. Ordinary closure, low demand, empty shelves or staff sickness do not turn native demand back on. These are real consequences of operating the business.

Handle new businesses, supported/unsupported menu changes, sale/removal and same-address replacement at bounded refresh points. Business identity is more than its address. Do not silently choose only the first eight eligible shops; use bounded local offer candidates and fair discovery/processing as the recognized scope grows.

A handoff freezes new commitments, retires/refunds only definitely unexecuted purchases and retains paid/uncertain receipts. Switch future customer generation at a verified native accounting boundary with no overlapping or retrospectively replayed demand. Existing native batches/customers and outstanding mod service must be accounted for. Stop mod sales immediately on loss of player ownership. If ownership cannot be resolved, visibly suspend the affected business until resolved, rather than inventing a safe transaction. Routine support transitions must complete automatically; exceptional suspension is not the normal onboarding flow.

The current disabled/opt-in implementation remains until this is built and tested. This planning task does not activate ordinary saves or deploy anything.

## Internal tuning, not player configuration
Keep a single small developer tuning profile: representation factor; job loss/find rates and support fraction; income/expense bands; modest food taste/meal preferences; car ownership and travel preferences; calendar pace (retain one-third initially). Keep rendering caps, cache limits and scheduler budgets separate as engineering constants. Save identity-affecting choices with the city. Balance values have names and simple meanings; avoid a framework of per-person/per-shop overrides or a large matrix of modes.

## Implementation sequence and finish gates
1. **One baseline and an early continuity check.** Consolidate this scope and the verified candidate; choose a new disposable development format and retire unnecessary lifecycle/mode paths. Preserve old evidence and payloads. Record a reference scene with real persistent traffic and show that tracked moving and parked cars retain identity through looking away/returning; test parked-car pickup after subway travel. The latest zero-car sample is insufficient coverage, not proof of a traffic bug: older traffic evidence exists. Fix a reproducible failure before adding economic scope. This planning task itself performs no game run.
2. **Ordinary operations and ownership at K=1.** Implement automatic whole-business ownership/discovery, uncertain-outcome handling and separate stock/staff feedback. Use practical shifts and actual purchased supplier replenishment through depletion/recovery over a few ordinary days. Check new/sold/replaced businesses and supported/unsupported menu edits at the ownership boundary, including pending/paid/uncertain receipt cases. Reuse native operations; no new logistics system. This establishes the conditions for a meaningful demand comparison, not a large market calibration campaign.
3. **One bounded consumer-factor experiment.** Implement K=2, shared valid walking quotes from the start and small stable tastes. Compare K=1 and K=2 under matched initialization/staffing/supply, then one price change for substitution. Test K=3 only after diagnosing K=2. Include a busy large store with verified native equipped/staffed throughput and a smaller or less favorable comparison where useful. A 75-capacity building alone is not proof of 75/hour service. Record hourly arrivals, service/lost demand, costs/cash and marginal route/decision work. Seek useful throughput above small-shop capacity at the busy location; no required full utilization or guaranteed profit. If 2–3x does not achieve that, diagnose opportunities, concentration, service and affordability rather than keep increasing K. Offline checks cover independent choices, unique receipts, money/stock/service, interrupted/overrun trips, uncertain holds and reload/cadence equivalence. K=1 with unchanged tastes must reproduce its pre-factor behavior; intentional taste or routing policy changes are compared separately. Stop on duplicate commerce, unexplained money or runaway routing, not by inventing a second simulator.
4. **Simple jobs, transport reuse and final play gate.** Add the small worker/seeking transitions and measured subway/route reuse as separate changes, retaining only optimizations that preserve route correctness and improve the observed bottleneck. Check income timing, workplace loss/return and live route invalidation while cars keep their identity/location. Complete one matched rendered commute/meal-peak comparison and one short hands-on management session on the integrated build. Include actual persistent cars at normal body budgets, one in-flight save/reload and ordinary fast-forward/catch-up. Reuse the operating evidence unless a later change affects it; do not repeat a complete campaign for each small edit.

Performance targets are developer acceptance criteria, not player settings. Before the comparisons, freeze the reference machine/modpack, scene, clock, graphics/body budgets, sample path and measurement definitions. Start with combined mod work p95 ≤5 ms/frame and no repeated mod-attributable >50 ms stalls during normal play; whole-frame p95 within 10% of a matched reference, with no new recurring mod-attributable >100 ms hitches. Compare K=2 against K=1 for total workload and catch-up, not per-decision time alone. Measure startup/reload and time-skip wait separately; set their numerical ceiling from the initial paired reference before tuning, publish it with the result and do not silently move it to pass. These are proposed targets to test, not established performance claims. A poor control does not excuse an unpleasant candidate: the hands-on session must also find the wait and frame pacing acceptable. A missed target needs an identified cause and a small fix or an explicit recorded scope/target decision, never a hidden waiver.

Release gate: persistent people/cars remain recognizable; each sale has one owner and native accounting; normal operation and eligibility transitions need no diagnostic intervention; demand creates understandable location/offer/capacity choices; declared performance/usability targets hold; and Peter's short hands-on test finds the feedback and pacing worthwhile. A failed gate triggers the smallest relevant fix, not more systems or endless new scenarios. Review consensus approves this scope, not performance, fun or publication. No ordinary-save deployment, upstream RLO change or release is performed by this planning task.
