## Confirmed defects and repairs

Five captured native body discards had zero velocity and an interior queue position whose permission equalled its held progress. Float32 placement projected the marker only 3.07–9.45 micrometres beyond permission. The old fixed nominal-minute tolerance rejected these stationary bodies. The new numerical helper requires a spatially substantiated error within four local float32 spacings, with a hard one-millimetre ceiling, and clamps to the **existing** permission. It does not enlarge traffic capacity or grant extra progress. Material movement, unsubstantiated endpoint credit, off-line geometry and invalid/coarse coordinates remain refusals.

A separate reproducible source defect reused a native corner receipt as movement on the following leg. For a right-angle route through `(0,0) → (10,0) → (10,10)`, the unchanged marker `(9,.5)` could satisfy native corner tolerance, then project half a metre along the next leg before admission. The adapter now records the marker at the exact native receipt. A bounded handoff holds that already-consumed pose until traffic admits it, without allowing additional forward motion or credit beyond current permission.

Independent Sol review found a further same-step ordering defect: native force and rotation calls precede the reached-waypoint check. Merely freezing the next step leaves an already-queued command free to move beyond the new receipt. Both owned commands now remain deferred until the native fixed-step postfix, when the latest receipt and traffic permission are known. The independent reproduction changed from roughly 20 centimetres of unintended advance to zero, with exactly one submitted force/rotation pair.

Native IL also skips both calls when no wheels are grounded. The fixed-step guard now stops retained horizontal drift and angular spin in that branch while preserving vertical force. Broken pairs or failed native steps stop the owned command and surface an adapter failure. Foreign bodies and native driving actions are not taken over. External contact/suspension impulses remain physical evidence to observe; the command guard is not a promise that collisions cannot move a body.

One original native receipt, c317, was moving about 12 m/s and appeared 6.14 centimetres beyond a newly exposed leg. Its old diagnostics do not attribute the cause. The corner and ordering mechanisms above are independently proven; they must not be presented as a proven explanation of this specific receipt.
