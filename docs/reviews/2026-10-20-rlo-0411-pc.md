# RLO 0.41.1 compatibility and PC rig installation — 20 October 2026

Status: private compatibility candidate accepted and installed on the PC test rig. All four source reviews, released-binary checks and the bounded Windows native smoke passed. Persistent City is enabled in the isolated test profile; the rig is stopped and released.

## Compatibility change

Persistent City 0.6.2 (schema 7) accepts the two individually reviewed RLO releases 0.40.4 and 0.41.1. Identity, physical reservations and temporary road-speed reads share the same exact assembly/informational-version gate. Crossed versions, unknown commits, missing metadata and unreviewed versions remain refused. The rest of each adapter's structural checks are unchanged. Runtime changes only its version metadata; the traffic, physical movement, persistence schema and economy behavior are unchanged from 0.6.1.

The historical `rlo:0.40.4:` population-key namespace and hash bytes remain unchanged. They identify the donor contract rather than the installed release. An existing city's donors still have to match every live identity field and the city seed. Valid upgrades therefore retain the original payload and assignments; actual donor changes refuse without rewriting the save.

## Observed limitations

No exceptions or Persistent City overshoots were reported. RLO's own diagnostics reported minimap outline bounds, incomplete one-way bus routes, bus stop-pole clearance and city-driver timing-budget failures. The first three appeared before Persistent City became physically active; the timing-budget failures appeared afterward. The campaign did not isolate their cause. These diagnostics are retained as unresolved observations, not dismissed as proven unrelated or treated as an automatic compatibility failure. Native RLO bus, social takeover/ragdoll, service-incident and active-pothole lifecycles were not exercised. Genuine clock-modifier 1→2→1 was not repeated on Windows; the prior Mac evidence remains separately attributed.

A diagnostic client initially read a native-speed receipt before completion. Waiting for its terminal receipt resolved that tooling race, after which the speed checks passed. A guarded file-copy attempt during asynchronous shutdown refused and was retried after the process stopped. Neither required a production change.
