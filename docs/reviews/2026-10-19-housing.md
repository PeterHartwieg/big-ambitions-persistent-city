# Historical whole-property housing experiment (superseded)

This report preserves version 0.3.0/schema 3 evidence for the 25,495-resident whole-property experiment. Version 0.3.1/schema 4 replaces that population policy with bounded rentable/buyable interior occupancy; see [the current interior report](2026-10-19-interiors.md). Historical performance, reviews and native checks below do not validate the current 957-resident fresh-city target.

This correction removes the RLO donor count as the population target. It remains a separate, RLO-dependent background prototype without visible actor replacement or economic writes. The density and occupancy values are provisional policy; the building areas are native data.

## Policy and implementation

- Eligible residential buildings use `Building.totalSqm`, the native whole-property area. `BuildingSizeData.squareMeters` describes the rentable interior and is used only as a conservative fallback when the whole-property value is absent. Unknown or undersized homes are omitted; the adapter never creates a native registration to obtain an area.
- Each home has `floor(area / 40 m²)` capacity. The city target is `floor(total capacity × 0.9)`, applied once globally so a one-person home does not automatically disappear. More than 50,000 residents refuses startup, rather than silently capping the requested city. This is a resource guard, not a 50,000-person performance claim.
- RLO's reviewed 400 profiles remain an immutable donor bank. Existing imported people retain their identities; additional residents get persistent, monotonically allocated `pc:<sequence>` IDs remixed from donor name parts, appearance and age-compatible personas. Source profiles are never changed.
- Established workers wait at least one day plus an exponential mean of 900 days before attempting a change. After a successful change or replacement arrival the minimum is 90 days plus that 900-day wait. An available different workplace and physical presence at home remain required. These are individual rates; aggregate changes grow with population. Future schema 2 job dates are stretched tenfold once; overdue events remain pending.
- Schema 3 preserves the existing storage key. Strict schema 1/2 validation precedes migration. Existing residents, home/day assignments, journeys and saved cars survive; expansion adds people. Legacy homes that cannot be rediscovered retain only already occupied capacity, and no unused historical allocation creates more housing. A reduced target never evicts existing residents.
- Native inputs are copied on the main thread. Pure population construction and strict saved-state decoding run on a worker. Publication rechecks the save object, payload, donor catalogue and clock. Unloaded work cannot publish into another save.
- Streaming shape validation avoids a full JSON DOM and preserves exact field/type/duplicate/range checks. Schema 1/2 retain their original 4 Mi-character, 5,000-person and 100,000 embedded-record bounds. Schema 3 permits up to 256 Mi characters, 50,000 residents and four million route/visit/opening records; these maxima cannot all be combined.
- `SaveSession` reuses validation only for a known immutable payload reference and bound dictionary. Changed payloads are strictly revalidated; the new snapshot is always validated. This removes a full previous-city decode from ordinary repeated saves. Serialization and the native disk save remain synchronous.

## Review and offline checks

204 tests pass and the native package compiles with warnings treated as errors. Astra, GPT 6.1 Sol, Grok 4.7 (resolved `grok-4.7-build`) and Claude Opus 5.5 approve the final source after fix rounds. Reviewers did not independently execute the native playthrough.

Confirmed findings fixed: Astra identified unused legacy capacity surviving discovery exclusion; Sol and Opus identified repeated full previous-payload decoding during save; Opus identified the original initialization payload remaining pinned after completion. Regression coverage includes occupied/empty omitted homes, a sole retained resident, exact-payload cache reuse and refusal of corrupt/future/foreign/replaced data. Other unproven observations were kept as test limitations rather than treated as defects.

The .NET 10 ARM64 benchmark uses the captured 400 donors and 1,007 places with simplified direct walking, not native routes. Creation took about 3.5–3.7 seconds of CPU time after donor caching, allocating approximately 1.4 GiB cumulatively. Fourteen synthetic days processed 2,476,907 events and retained 727,168 visits. A mature snapshot was 95.17 MB UTF-8, wrote in about 0.92 seconds and strictly read in about 2.05 seconds. These are not Unity/Mono or frame-rate measurements.

A separate persistence stress case with 25,495 residents, 32 visits each and 20% travelling on continuous 150-leg car routes produced 169,913,243 JSON characters (162.0 MiB), wrote in 1.55 seconds and read in 6.80 seconds. Maximum measured process RSS was about 1.29 GiB; cumulative allocations were much larger and do not represent retained memory. The session cache avoids repeating that old-payload decode on normal saves, but large save/load costs remain a limitation.

## Native evidence

Isolated headless Mac rig only: Big Ambitions build 3682, bridge runtime 6 and released RLO 0.40.4. The normal Mac profile and Windows PC were not used.

The native inventory found 363 residential controllers; 335 passed the current access/entrance filters and 28 were excluded by the pedestrian-spawn flag. Eligible whole-property area totals 1,138,731 m², compared with 37,400 m² of rentable interior area. All eligible homes supplied whole-property area, so no fallback was needed. The resulting capacity is 28,328 and the experimental target is 25,495 residents. This averages about 76 people per eligible building. Median whole-property area is 1,442 m², but the largest reported value is 82,329 m², so a few large properties contribute heavily. These economic property-area fields do not establish usable residential floor area or apartment counts.

The user questioned the resulting scale. For sensitivity only, applying the same 90% rule with 80 m²/person gives 12,674 and 120 m²/person gives 8,392. Using only known rentable interiors with the original 40 m² policy gives 730. These scenarios describe different assumptions; none is a verified city census. The 25,495-resident run is a capacity experiment, not a settled population default. That design decision has since been resolved in favour of rentable/buyable interiors with bounded occupant bands. The scenarios above remain historical sensitivity calculations, not current policy.

An intermediate housing build (`221bc0f…`) migrated a genuine native schema 2 checkpoint. All 400 original identities and their home/day/shop/leisure assignments were preserved, the entire donor catalogue was unchanged, and 25,095 generated residents were added. A two-game-hour normal-speed morning run finished caught up. Five-second samples peaked at 12.30 game minutes of backlog and 45.86 ms per advancement call. Catch-up finished about ten seconds after the first paused observation. These timings are not total-frame or rendering measurements.

Final native build: SHA-256 `41bd40f3663b3a027857611d126b2da341018ee67f83d1ab54fc86f1fdfaf428`, assembly version 0.3.0.0.

The final build reloaded the migrated checkpoint. Independent core replay of the native clock's 0.333333-minute reload advance matched the entire canonical state exactly without requesting a new route. It retained all 551 logical cars and 623 continuing journeys; 23 journeys arrived during that small advance. The saved payload was 26,629,124 characters and the compressed native checkpoint about 4.31 MB.

Two explicit native saves on the final build took 2.144 and 1.865 seconds end to end, including bridge and native save work. The stored-payload validation count remained one across both saves, confirming that the real game exercised the cache fast path. These are young-city snapshots, not mature-save timings or isolated serializer costs.

The final full-day 6× test **failed to keep pace**. At the final observation after 633.725 seconds, the simulation clock was 2,542 and game time was 3,483.010245, leaving 941.010 game minutes of backlog; the observed maximum was 997.308. The run recorded 30,288 completed journeys, 3,127 missed activities and 4,859 allocated cars. The maximum measured advancement call was 337.9056 ms. The game was paused and the test ended without the simulation catching up. These are advancement-loop elapsed times, not total-frame or CPU measurements. This experiment does not establish stable throughput at 25,495 residents.

Cleanup restored and verified the original installation manifest and file hashes. All 22 preexisting save files were unchanged. The rig was released. These cleanup checks apply to this completed historical experiment; the separate 0.3.1 interior-policy validation has its own evidence record.

## Remaining limits

The old 16 assignments per business/study role remains provisional. At this population it leaves 15,842 residents without work/study assignments; this is a major policy limitation. Housing capacity is now independent of donor count, but employment/school capacity is not yet a convincing city model. Native employment and the economy remain untouched.

Frozen venue schedules, reachability-blind destination assignment, non-preemptible path events, low-resolution transport and lack of visible actor replacement remain. Density, occupancy and lifecycle timing need playtesting. Rendering, Windows, long native soaks, mature native save costs and 50,000-resident throughput are unverified. The private package is disabled by default.
