## Implemented behavior

Every resident car on a mapped native road reserves its place in one directed section, whether visible or distant. Sections coalesce compatible native edges up to 80 metres, preserving junctions, branches, merges, lane/access and speed boundaries. Capacity is a provisional six metres per car and discharge headway is 1.8 native seconds. Native periodic signal data supplies background phases; native physics, signals and avoidance still control visible bodies. These are section queues, not citywide rigidbody simulation or a calibrated multi-lane traffic model.

Finite storage causes waiting and downstream spillback. Sources retain FIFO demand. An interior curb source waits for an empty section under the conservative first policy. A final curb exit can leave before the far-end junction once preceding cars have passed its point with spacing. It does not consume that junction's signal or headway. No anonymous trips are added to create congestion. RLO scenes, native customers/workers and service vehicles remain the previously agreed exceptions; their distant road occupancy is not represented in these queues.

The event calendar stores one next wakeup per demanded section. City and traffic events share one logical cursor; bounded catch-up does not jump over overdue events. Physical ownership changes preserve queue identity and spatial progress. A visible head that has not reached its permitted point holds followers. Saving synchronizes positions and records the queue, source order, motion clocks, signal phase and calendar. Reload releases physical ownership into the same background queues. Native fast-forward uses background cars and resumes physical bodies at normal speed.

A route's geometry keeps nominal travel duration while a separate transient estimate includes queue delays. Walking, available subway journeys and available-car journeys compete on complete estimated door-to-door minutes, including access/egress, preparation, expected station wait and virtual parking. Exact ties retain stable candidate order. Preference/fare fields remain readable for compatibility but do not outweigh faster arrival. Car location/ownership and access constraints still determine feasibility.

Road searches use a current departure-time queue snapshot, not arrival-time predictions at every edge. Station/curb search counts remain bounded (nearest two station/curb candidates and at most two car road searches); shortest means the best feasible evaluated candidate. Journeys already underway remain committed. A bounded interior-entry estimate includes the conservative empty-section rule. It projects only current committed demand, with at most 32 sections, 64 vehicles, 1,024 inspected references and 256 events. Exceeding those bounds conservatively makes that car candidate unavailable; it is not evidence of a proven road closure. Projected physical cars extrapolate from measured progress at nominal speed, without changing live physical ownership. New arrivals and future physical stops remain unknown. A proven closed capacity cycle is unavailable to new routes; existing vehicles remain conserved. There is no overflow or teleport rescue. Mode changes can reduce new car demand, but cannot guarantee that every existing gridlock clears.

## Performance changes and offline measurements

Native metadata capture and publication checks are staged at 2,048 work units and a cooperative two-millisecond target. Pure graph construction runs on a worker using copied data, never Unity/native calls. Every captured contract is revalidated before publication; no population events advance against partial infrastructure. Junction checks are memoized within a synchronous query. Existing route search bounds and final native path validation remain.

Snapshot rebasing now occurs once after each frame's event batch, rather than after every one-event slice. A frame permits up to 128 total events with at most sixteen resident activity events, still using the soft two-millisecond advancement budget. A single native route query or GC pause can exceed that cooperative budget. Diagnostics include snapshot publication but are not whole-frame/FPS measurements.

Synthetic fixture: 18,432 nodes in 96 disjoint corridors, coalesced into 1,440 sections, with 957 committed commuters. This is an artificial stress workload, not a native city measurement. The kernel, batch and cap results below were rerun on source `c7d9ebae…983b0`. The later physical-span correction changes only the runtime adapter; the measured Core code is unchanged. Earlier development results remain separately retained.

- Kernel-only drain: 14,643 events, 957 exact completions, 13.67 ms and 5.05 MB allocated in one measured run.
- Twelve paired runs comparing per-slice publication with once-per-batch publication retained identical per-frame event/clock traces and final state. Median time for 1,000 frames improved 4.70× at 1× with background cars and 13.49× at 6×; scenarios holding fifty physical leaders improved 2.79× and 7.00×. Allocation totals were effectively unchanged. These comparisons retained the old sixteen-total-event cap and omitted the wall-clock cutoff to isolate publication overhead.
- A separate event-cap comparison reduced synthetic peak 6× backlog from 151.2 to 2.1 game minutes with background cars, and from 12.3 to 0.3 with fifty held leaders. Event totals and final states matched. It also omitted the wall-clock cutoff, so it does not predict native lag or FPS.
- An isolated staged native-adapter harness used 18,432 stub nodes, verified no worker native reads or premature publication, revalidated every captured base contract and rejected a mutation during construction. Largest observed Prepare call was 5.04 ms despite the cooperative 2 ms target; scheduling/GC can overrun it. The bounded entry forecast measured 0.356 ms mean / 1.124 ms maximum and 187,392 bytes per near-bound query in a 50-call isolated probe. Empty/boundary-entry fast paths allocated zero bytes across 20,000 queries. These are pure-model observations, not a native frame benchmark.

## Review and fix rounds

Reviewers are GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, Grok 4.7 and Claude Opus 5.5. Findings are independently evaluated; reviewer suggestions are not assumed correct.

Confirmed corrections include dormant closed-road reopening, pending car-access routes refusing changed topology, coalesced-section clock conversion/reload, exact curb departures affected by floating-point subtraction, partial destination curb discharge, high-clock signal wakeup tolerance, and near-end retiming without crediting unobserved physical arrival. Binding validates native section geometry, intermediate corners, retained tags, complete remaining road coverage and actual movement duration. A further Sol reproduction showed that an unlabelled retimed final edge could have incomplete queue coverage despite valid individual claims. New clock splits now retain explicit native-edge fragment provenance and repeated conversions preserve it; ordinary curb connectors remain separate. Pre-schema-7 journeys can contain older unlabelled fragments and keep their previously declared migration behavior. Conversion of a partial native edge already at the 512-leg limit refuses atomically, including before queue admission, rather than storing mismatched timing.

Grok subsequently found that retained fragment tags prevented physical sampling of every later full edge in the road run. An initial proposal to reset the physical span after a fragment was rejected when Sol found that it forgot prior junction-exit rear clearance; Astra independently confirmed that case. The final sampler keeps validated historical fragments for their actual geometric distances and verified native endpoint boundaries, but never adds fragments to driving targets. Missing entry/exit evidence remains a refusal. The persistent offline harness in `tools/road-handoff-regression/` compiles the actual Runtime adapter against synthetic native surfaces and verifies later full-edge sampling, prior-exit clearance despite a future balanced junction, clearance recovery, distinct junction identities, and rejection of malformed metadata. Opus then identified a sub-millimetre endpoint split that could duplicate a boundary and permanently withhold a body. Consecutive observations of one native node now retain the earliest entry and latest exit until another node intervenes; this only delays clearance. The strengthened two-junction fixture isolates the previous exit as the sole reason for refusal, and tiny-split checks cover both refusal and recovery. Restoring the old deduplication rule fails the new regression. Astra and Sol independently ran the final harness and approved both corrections.

Rejected alternatives: cancelling queues and letting saved cars proceed nominally after a structural topology change would silently bypass the model. Capacity overflow to dissolve a full cycle would likewise break conservation. Structural changes instead stop this adapter once and preserve the payload for reviewed migration; a successor-array reorder can also trigger that conservative fingerprint policy. The normal temporary-closure/reopening path remains supported.

Physical progress is bounded by current owned storage. Transfer to the next section is processed by the event loop after an endpoint report; permission never grants occupancy that has not been acquired. This can impose a frame-boundary handoff. The limiter is a per-physics-step displacement cap, not a long braking-distance stop at every section. Its visual impact still needs native observation. The native owned-body wrapper pairs the verified AddForce and MoveRotation instructions, constrains the entire front-marker rotation arc, and reserves its forward displacement before limiting translation. Pure tests include a stopped interior limit and a turn whose intermediate arc advances farther than its final pose. An offline check against actual local FixedUpdate IL replaces exactly one pair and rejects a changed instruction shape. External collision impulses remain separately observed evidence; overshoot never grants logical queue progress.
