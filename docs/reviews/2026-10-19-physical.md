# Physical population prototype (0.4.0)

Persistent City remains an independent, RLO-dependent, non-economy mod. The accepted first physical version preserves RLO scenes and native customers/workers/service vehicles, uses native movement pace without changing the game clock, and uses virtual destination parking. It is disabled by default.

## Implementation

A bounded view assigns native bodies to existing residents and their committed journeys. It creates neither filler identities nor random destinations. Visible movement controls route progress; blocked pedestrians and cars delay their arrivals. Releasing a body resumes background travel at its last measured point. Cars retain their own model, paint and logical curb location without creating native vehicle/save ownership.

Walking follows saved native NavMesh corners. Car edges retain the native topology fingerprint and directed waypoint IDs; ordered native waypoint notifications and measured geometry substantiate progress. Traffic lights and avoidance remain native. Pedestrian appearances derive from saved identities. Ordinary native street spawning is intercepted after preflight; existing ordinary bodies retire out of view. An initial ordinary pedestrian reaching its endpoint while watched waits there rather than receiving another random route.

Defaults are 48 pedestrian bodies, 24 vehicle bodies and a 120-metre selection radius. Available native ordinary vehicle slots may be fewer. Virtual parking includes hidden curb/preparation/parking transitions; occupied parking spaces, parking manoeuvres and boarding animations are not implemented. Subway travel remains hidden between station walks. Genuine game-clock modifier changes can leave the remainder of the current split road edge in background simulation until its next whole native edge; native 1×/6× speed changes do not retime the committed route, but native fast-forward now releases physical bodies for background travel.

Schema 5 persists native timing metadata and car appearance. Strict schemas 1–4 migrate without replacing identities or current journey positions. Only pre-schema-4 saves receive housing enrichment. Explicit invalid dates remain rejected; narrow tolerances handle floating-point subtraction/readdition at completed arrivals. Stationary waits retain game minutes while moving travel retimes.

## Review and corrections

Astra, GPT 6.1 Sol, Grok 4.7 and Claude Opus 5.5 inspected the source in fix rounds. Findings were checked against current source and native contracts. Accepted fixes include fractional corner/arrival rounding, physical adapter failure isolation, fair acquisition retries, crossing permission latches, constant-time lease ownership checks, native vehicle slot preflight, private-driver takeover protection, and ambient pedestrian recycle interception/restoration. Native first-pass evidence also found a lateral-avoidance stop latch and the wrong ambient visibility predicate; both were corrected.

Not every suggestion was adopted. Timing out a visibly blocked journey would invent movement through an obstruction, so visible blockage retains the lease while path retries are bounded. Private-driver takeover does reset the native preset path; explicit ownership checks were added as additional protection. HUD speed changes cancel out of the observed clock-rate ratio and do not cause road retiming. RLO's new Flare invitation path copies the canonical avatar seed into its social person; tuple reservation is valid for that path, but old contacts and name collisions have no universal citizen foreign key. Full identity unification remains deferred.

All four reviewers approve the waypoint gate, signal-coverage correction and expired collision-stop correction in source, conditional on native validation. Opus suggested tolerating orphan-only cleanup failures; the prototype deliberately retains the conservative reload/restart requirement when it cannot restore coverage safely. Its grid-list mutation hypothesis was checked against native GridManager: changed membership creates a replacement list and raises the active-intersections event. Full-route pins can reduce physical car capacity at the 128-junction bound; acquisition is refused rather than omitting required signals. Native activation resets newly active junction occupancy, as it does during ordinary camera-grid activation; broader foreign-vehicle scenarios remain unverified.

Current source passes 356 offline tests and compiles against the isolated Mac assemblies with warnings treated as errors. Tests cover exclusive leases, measured/blocked progress, fractional boundaries, vertical walking, early commute timing, save migration and rejection, arrival roundoff, and read-only RLO reservations. The suite is separate from native gameplay evidence.

## Native validation and diagnostic history

The isolated Mac rig uses build 3682, runtime 6, Metal/background graphics and RLO 0.40.4. The normal profile and Windows rig were not used. Fresh initialization confirmed 957 residents, 335 eligible interiors and capacity 1,064.

The first two native runs found rounding/latch defects. The second failure restored native ambient spawning and retained the saved own payload exactly. They are diagnostic evidence, not successful end-to-end validation.

A corrected third run loaded the interrupted checkpoint, advanced from day 1 10:29 to 11:30 at 1×, and saved successfully. Samples showed up to 48 bound pedestrians and four cars, actual displacement alongside measured route progress, no sampled progress regression, and later completed-journey counters for previously bound residents. Ordinary ambient pedestrian/vehicle candidates reached zero; taxis remained active. Native saved vehicle count stayed zero. A screenshot was inspected. These samples do not establish continuously visible complete itineraries, traffic-light compliance in every situation, or a frame-rate benchmark.

A fourth run preserved the saved payload, all 957 identities/assignments, 400 source identities, 1,009 places, 45 assigned car appearances and all 40 comparable pedestrian fingerprints across reload. During its longer accelerated-clock test, cars progressively left intersections stuck red. Native source tracing found repeated same-target waypoint requests registering duplicate intersection entries. That run is failed traffic evidence despite an active background simulation; the request gate was corrected for subsequent clean-load runs. The bridge's speed 6 changes game minutes per real second while physics remains at timeScale 1; this is distinct from native activity fast-forward.

The fifth run completed the accelerated-clock day with no duplicate/inactive/unknown occupants in the active-junction samples, but it still exposed prolonged red queues at 143–167 metres from the player. Native signals update only inside camera-adjacent traffic grid cells, a smaller area than the 168-metre car retirement radius. This run is therefore diagnostic evidence, not a clean traffic pass. A sixth run directly confirmed four stopped cars targeting inactive junctions (Intersection_5, Intersection_16 and Intersection_33), with signal clocks frozen at zero while the manager clock exceeded 118 seconds. The correction pins the actual native intersections on committed physical car paths, capped at 128 distinct pinned junctions including baseline coverage. It copies the requested native baseline and supplies a deduplicated union, retains pins through native vehicle removal, and restores the latest baseline after release. Signal timing remains native. Orphaned foreign occupants retain coverage; incomplete cleanup explicitly requires reload/restart. The seventh native run checked this correction as described below.

The seventh run confirmed signal cycling, zero inactive required junctions, correct pin accounting, camera-move continuity and baseline restoration. It also exposed an unrelated expired collision-stop latch: a preset-path car retained native StopNow with a negative timer for 70 seconds while its signal cycled. Native random cars clear that expired action; preset-path cars do not. The correction removes only that expired native StopNow for valid non-final owned cars, preserving other native actions and fresh collision stops. Subsequent runs checked queue recovery without a camera release. The native expiry branch was checked directly: it only removes StopNow via NewDriveActionArrived; the correction restores that same operation.

The installed Food Trucks 0.10.0 bridge lacks its newer activity fast_forward operation. A disposable cart fixture confirmed that limitation; it is not part of the city-only checkpoint or package. Native outdoor 6× was instead exercised through an explicit isolated test control calling the normal GameSpeedController API, separately from read-only snapshots. This tests the controller path, not an activity UI integration.

The eighth fixed-camera run completed the accelerated-clock day and caught up at the final paused sample; sampled backlog peaked at 155.81 game minutes. It recorded three expired-stop recoveries and independently sampled one same-body StopNow-to-moving transition with measured progress. No negative StopNow latch persisted in the samples. Other long queues remained, so this is still not a clean traffic pass. Source tracing then identified acquisition clearing freshly installed native red/yield actions and allowing bodies to appear within junction traversals without entry admission. Both were corrected before pass 9. A further source mismatch delayed native steering: the game uses GetFrontAxle, while the adapter placed the pivot at the route point, tested centre of mass and reported pivot progress. Acquisition now aligns the marker in XZ while retaining native vertical placement; marker position drives both waypoint proof and progress. The corrected admission checks consume the same four-attempt budget even when they fail. All four reviewers approve this source delta, conditional on the repeat native evidence. Opus identified two intentional coverage costs: an oversized overlap box can reduce acquisition on dense streets, and empty/unclosed junction boundary sequences keep an entire road block in the background, including short junction-free drives. These conservative restrictions are retained for this private prototype; nearby physical coverage is not guaranteed. Pure tests cover boundary clearance and two model offsets. Extra read-only wheel/contact diagnostics accompany pass 9.

Native controller fast-forward was exercised separately: five settled snapshots had timeScale 6, travelClockRate 1 and native traffic enabled. Sampled movement plus measured progress included 44 walking and 12 car pairs, without regression. The initial transition sample was rebuilding the adapter; all six runtime samples remained healthy. This validates the public controller path, not an activity UI integration. The native controller was returned to normal and the rig paused, saved to a new diagnostic checkpoint, and stopped.

Pass 9 completed the fixed-camera 24-hour accelerated-clock run (native physics at 1×), with 957 residents and zero lag at the final paused sample. Across 29 captures, peak bodies were 48 pedestrians and 17 cars; 243 distinct residents appeared. There were 817 walking and 169 driving same-body sample pairs with both displacement and measured progress, without regression. Sampled backlog peaked at 40.004 game minutes. Two sampled repeated stationary car episodes lasted about 13 seconds and both later moved on the same body/lease. Required signal coverage, occupancy accounting and target/path-head checks found no anomalies. The final clock-policy reset cleared bodies and is not evidence of traffic recovery. Strict normal/fast physics diagnostics covered 15 car models, with measured position matching their native front marker and sampled wheel traction present.

The queues persisted after returning to 1×. Sparse observations do not isolate the initial cause between coarse physics stepping and target handling. The earlier broad native6 movement observations must not be interpreted as a clean traffic pass. ## Remaining scope

The city still uses frozen venue/assignment data and provisional work/study capacity. Longer natural travel can miss activity windows; this phase does not overhaul the economy or solve every schedule. Public scene/customer/worker/service exceptions prevent a completely closed population. Full visible parking, a citizen inspector, household relationships, live venue refresh, populated-economy integration, long soaks and Windows performance remain future work. Fun requires user playtesting.
