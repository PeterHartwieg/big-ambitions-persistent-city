# Native price precision and accounting migration

Decision: Peter selected Q3 A on 28 October 2026. Resident payments and receipts must retain native price precision; changing native prices or loosening the financial gate is not authorized.

## Read-only installed-assembly evidence

Inspected `BigAmbitions.dll` in the Steam installation?s `Big Ambitions_Data/Managed` directory using the existing local ILSpy CLI. Assembly SHA-256: `9427561b81aaab2ada09c22a3c98dd9a4017a65225b389513885bf336852e911`. `RetailPrice.price` and `Entities.OrderEntry.price` are `System.Single` (IEEE 754 binary32), in dollars. `ItemHelper.GetPrice` returns the matching retail float directly; `RetailBusinessSimulator` assigns it to the order and asks the citizen to approve it. That path has no cent rounding. Native statement values exposed by our adapter are also floats.

Only source findings and the assembly hash are recorded here. Extracted inspection text stays in ignored `build/pc-decisions/`; no game assembly, decompiled implementation or raw save enters Git. The game was not launched and its files were not changed.

## Unit and rounding

Review round 7 (fix for round-6 findings): resident money is an exact whole number of **units**, `Money` in `Core/FoodMoney.cs`, a checked signed 64-bit count of 1 unit = 2^-28 cents = 2^-30/25 dollars. The decided unit was 2^-30 dollars; that unit cannot hold a whole cent ($0.01 = 1/(2^2 * 5^2) dollars is not a multiple of 2^-30), and income, housing, essentials, home food, allowances and every schema-13 balance are arbitrary whole cents. 2^-28 cents is the smallest unit that holds both every supported binary32 price and every cent exactly: each 2^-30-dollar count is 25 units, and each cent is 2^28 units. Wallet balances, holds, debits, refunds, postings, prepared-food spend, receipts, shop/product history, allowances, shadow offers/choices and every persisted amount use it. Fixed policy rates and preference coefficients remain integer cents and convert with `Money.FromCents`. The choice between this unit and the decided 2^-30 dollars has been escalated to the technical lead (review round 8); the unit is unchanged until that decision.

Every `+`, `-`, `*` and `Money.Sum` is overflow-checked (`OverflowException`), and nothing rounds. Headroom is about $343.6 million per amount: a top-band resident ($500/day) needs roughly 687,000 game days to reach it in lifetime income, and one shop-day of history would need over 34,000 maximum-price ($10,000) sales. Cross-resident diagnostic totals (management panel, `FoodEconomyInsight`) are summed exactly as unbounded integers and shown as exact cents text or whole-dollar display; display never changes a settlement amount. Scores convert to double for utility comparisons only, and those doubles are exactly the earlier decimal-to-double values.

`Money.FromNative` decodes the binary32 fields: `units = m * 25 * 2^(biased-120)`. Every price of at least 2^-7 dollars (about $0.0078), and so every price of a cent or more, is a whole unit count. The refusals are unchanged: NaN, infinity, nonpositive, subnormal, above $10,000, and sub-2^-7 prices that fall between units. That is exactly the set the decimal conversion refused for needing more than 28 fractional digits (`biased + trailing zero bits < 120` in both). An exhaustive test checks every positive normal binary32 value up to $10,000 (about 1.18 billion) against an independent double oracle.

For `$10.2528f` (bits `1092881272`) the charge is `275221504000` units = `1025.279998779296875` cents, exactly the native value. 109 charges total `$1117.55519866943359375` and 110 total `$1127.8079986572265625`, the exact rational sum of the native order prices. Wallet, receipt and history arithmetic carry that integer unchanged, and confirmation and refusal never round again.

Native sums still use native float arithmetic. The financial gate has zero tolerance: each resident payment equals its order's native price bit for bit, and resident totals equal the exact sum of the native order prices. The game's own float32 running totals drift by native accumulation; that drift is recorded as rig evidence and attributed to the game. A native campaign must verify stock, bags, receipts, wallets, history, accounts and cash together. The historical 30.52-cent failure is not rewritten as a pass.

Before funds move or native checkout starts, checkout checks that every later addition fits: resident spend, the daily allowance, shop and product history (`CanSettle`), and prepared-food spend (`HoldPurchase`, which returns false rather than throwing). A refused purchase is Missed with its wallet untouched, so no addition in immediate settlement can fail after a native sale. One residual is not guarded (declined as overguarding): a receipt confirmed later (Uncertain to Paid) adds to its checkout day's history row, and other sales may use that row's headroom meanwhile. Overflow there needs about $343.6 million of revenue in one shop-day; if it happened, reconciliation could leave the wallet confirmed with a partial history update, or stop the food step.

`PostDay` computes every change, including the wage refreshed from the resident, before assigning any. It also checks the total that save validation sums, opening savings plus income received, which can overflow when each amount fits. An overflow throws and leaves the budget unchanged, so an accepted posting always leaves a savable ledger. Holds, confirmations and refunds move money between accounted amounts and leave both conservation totals unchanged. Save validation checks conservation with the same checked arithmetic (`HouseholdFinance.CashConserved`), alongside every existing conservation, identity, journey and ownership invariant.

## Checkout and receipts

The decision cache remains unchanged. Checkout reads live offers, compares the exact unit price, checks it again after native setup and before creating a receipt, and requires exact native float equality in the price-acceptance hook and post-sale order evidence. A one-ULP price change, even one far below a cent, cannot use stale approval. Native prices, orders and statement arithmetic remain native-owned.

New markers use `persistentcity:food-sale-v2:` with canonical invariant decimal cents in the existing pending/paid identity payload. `Money.ToCentsText` writes the exact cents of the unit count, the same text the decimal implementation wrote for the same amount, so existing v2 markers keep their identity. The reader recognizes unchanged v1 integer-cent markers for migrated intentions. Pending/unknown/mismatching evidence still blocks retry; simultaneous v1/v2 markers conflict and survive pruning. Unresolved old markers remain protected. There is no retroactive price upgrade or historical debit.

## Save compatibility

City schema stays **14**: it is unreleased, and the field names now identify the money unit. Current payloads write money as canonical JSON integer unit counts in fields without the old suffix (`Balance`, `Price`, `Revenue`, ...). Shape checking validates each money token from its original text before typed deserialization: it must be a canonical 64-bit integer, so fractions, exponents, leading zeros (which Json.NET would read as octal) and out-of-range values are Invalid, never rounded. The check reads the whole token, back to the preceding JSON delimiter or whitespace, so Json.NET's hexadecimal form (`0x10`) and any other prefixed syntax are Invalid too. The same check covers migrated `...Cents` fields and shadow documents. Valid payloads round-trip byte-identically.

Schema **13** (whole-cent `...Cents` fields) and earlier schema-14 development payloads (decimal-cent `...Cents` fields, including the 28-digit exact form) migrate in memory from their original token text. Each amount converts only if it is exactly a whole number of units within range; otherwise the whole payload is Invalid and preserved. Earlier schema-14 saves with twelve-digit cents, such as `1025.279998779297`, are therefore refused, because those amounts are not whole units. Exact binary32 amounts and whole cents convert exactly. Old field names are recognized with or without whitespace before the colon. A payload that mixes old and new money fields is refused. Migration never writes the source payload or buys anything. It retains identities, balances, agreed prices, meal/traffic phases, holds and histories, and the next successful save publishes the unit form. Invalid, foreign, future and pre-13 experimental payloads remain preserved and refused.

Receipts: v1 integer-cent markers still match migrated whole-cent intentions, and v2 markers keep their exact decimal-cent identity. Paid/pending/unknown evidence keeps its agreed amount and still blocks retry. Simultaneous v1/v2 markers conflict and survive pruning. An unexecuted intention whose stored price differs from the exact live price is refused and refunded by the existing price-change flow before any receipt or native sale.

Detached food-shadow documents stay at schema **5** under the same rules: schemas 1-4 (whole cents) and earlier schema-5 documents (decimal cents) migrate exactly or are refused. The shadow remains hypothetical. Outside the codecs, plain Json.NET serializes money as `{"Units": n}` and round-trips it exactly. Current whole numbers no longer serialize with `.0`.

## Evidence boundaries

Offline tests cover the round-6 regressions: cheap odd prices ($0.01-$0.24) through first checkout, exact day-end posting after a high-precision purchase, exact shop/product history, a top-band wallet through day 20,000, a $1M wallet holding $1.37, and over-precise save tokens. Round-8 tests add hexadecimal tokens in current, schema-13, earlier schema-14 and shadow fields, a posting whose conservation total would exceed the save range (and its exact-boundary control), an atomic posting with a wage refresh, and earlier schema-14 migration with spaced colons. They also cover exhaustive conversion, mixed-magnitude sums, settlement refusal before hold or native checkout, atomic posting overflow, the 109/110-sale gate, v1/v2 receipt compatibility, schema-13 and earlier schema-14 migration, and wire-name uniqueness. Actual adapter harnesses cover fractional checkout, one-ULP changes, late price mutation, stock and idempotent replay against native doubles. These are source/offline tests, not native financial gate clearance. A separately authorized game run remains required. Historical rig verifier scripts under `docs/reviews/rig` and the experiment tools' plot scripts read the earlier `...Cents` field names and were not changed.
