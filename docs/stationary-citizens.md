# Stationary citizens: approved optional stops

Peter's 28 October answers in `persistent-city-DECISIONS-2.md` approve all five proposed optional kinds. They supersede the earlier proposal and its unresolved questions. Q1 is **leave street crowds and routines unchanged now**; time/weather-aware routines are a separate future feature.

| Kind | Existing eligible activity | Game minutes | Rain |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Individual break | Employee already admitted at an open workplace; mid-shift opportunity | 5–10 | Cancel in any rain |
| Window shopping | Existing admitted shopping visit at an open shop | 1–3 | Cancel in any rain |
| Resting | Existing park visit, at a validated park edge | 10–40 | Cancel in any rain |
| Chatting | Two existing eligible visitors at the same nightlife venue | 10–30 | Cancel in any rain |
| Performer audience | Persistent pedestrian passing a live performer while arriving on an existing walk | 2–8 | Cancel in any rain |

No minimum crowd, anonymous audience, extra nightlife outing, replacement citizen or native sale is created. The existing quiet-hour and trip-generation rules remain authoritative. A late-night stop can use an already eligible visit; it cannot create one. Native anonymous stationary zones stay suppressed. Performers and the native exceptions already retained by `StationaryCrowds` remain unchanged.

## Approved frequency and QA configuration

DECISIONS-3 approves a **25%** default. Since Peter's option-B decision of 29 October the setting is the **target share of eligible visits that visibly get a real scheduled optional stop**, not a per-visit choice probability applied before the safety filters. **0** means no new optional stops at all. **100** means every eligible visit that has at least one feasible stop gets one: the maximum feasible share. Any value, and in practice every high value, is capped by feasibility; a visit with no feasible candidate (no validated geometry, too little time, occupied spots, no conversation partner) never gets a stop. The pre-option-B direct-draw sample yielded 98/317 (about 31%); the 29 October option-B campaign measured 4,392/10,162 = 43.22% at 100. These are observed outputs for their respective samples, not a universal saturation threshold; weather and unsaved geometry sampling vary between arms. The default delivers **about 25% long-run**: over N eligible visits, realised share = (draws - (closingPending - openingPending) - drops) / N. Draws are unbiased (25% in expectation); 0.25 +/- 1.96*sqrt(0.1875/N) is an approximate 95% binomial reference across seed samples, not a guaranteed interval for one fixed deterministic seed, and the rig draw sample lies outside it. Pending chances are bounded at one per resident (957 on this rig), and no drops occurred at 25%. Sampled peaks were 209 in the initial traced default arm, 211 in its continued audit and 245 in the untraced `light25-summary.json`; these are observations, not universal ceilings. The cold shortfall is mostly a startup lag; any window differs from its draw share by delta-pending/N and drops/N, where delta-pending is closing minus opening stock. With zero opening stock and zero drops, that lag was 174/10,037 = 1.7 points after the rig's first ten days and 99/32,785 = 0.3 points in its 28-day untraced run. Pending stock also cycles later: the warm continuation realised 18 more stops than it drew as stock fell from 174 to 156. Here an eligible visit, or trip, is a newly admitted visit to an open shop, workplace or leisure place, outside rain, meal outings and food-owned shops; home and study visits are not eligible. Runtime reads `OptionalStopChoicePercentage` from the existing `persistent-city.json` in `ModContext.ModRootPath`: installed as `<game directory>/ModsLocal/PersistentCity/persistent-city.json`. The section is the **top-level JSON object**, not a nested section. The source default is `config/persistent-city.json`; the build copies it to `build/PersistentCity/persistent-city.json`.

The key keeps its existing name `OptionalStopChoicePercentage` so existing configs keep working; only its meaning changed. Use an integer from **0 through 100** (default **25**). Negative values clamp to 0 and values above 100 clamp to 100. Missing, non-numeric, null, boolean, object/array or in-range fractional values fall back to 25; numeric strings are accepted. Invalid JSON syntax still follows the existing configuration failure path. Edit while the city is unloaded and reload the mod/city; there is no live file watcher.

Only newly admitted visits use the updated setting. A saved acceptance or decline survives events that retain the same admitted visit, and a pending chance survives later events and reloads until it is spent (or, after the setting is lowered, trimmed to the new bound; see below); none is re-rolled; active stop deadlines and cancellation remain authoritative even at rate 0. Camera moves and route rebuilds do not affect choices. No QA reset path was added: use a later ordinary visit or a disposable fresh city to observe a new rate. Each visit is evaluated once at admission and at most one audience chance per existing journey, with worker stops timed mid-shift. This enables production scheduling in source; it is not native playtest or release clearance.

## Scheduling and continuation

`Core/OptionalStopScheduling.cs` evaluates a newly admitted visit once, after its ordinary event commits. Stable citizen/place/visit keys draw the visit's chance and duration. Each newly admitted eligible visit (trip) draws one chance at the setting, with the unchanged stable hash. A drawn visit tries an audience stop, then a conversation, then its own kind, under every safety filter. A chance that its visit cannot use stays **pending** on a citizen (saved `OptionalStopChoice = true` with no stop): across the end of that visit, later events, saves and reloads, until a feasible visit spends it. That is either the holder's own later eligible visit, drawn or not, or a **hand-on**: an undrawn eligible visit anywhere in the city that has a feasible stop takes one pending chance, gets the stop and records an acceptance, and the holder's choice becomes a decline. Hand-ons take holders that are not waiting at a leisure venue first, then holders waiting at one (possible conversation partners); within each group the lowest ordinal stable citizen identity goes first. An undrawn leisure arrival that takes a chance can also start a conversation when at least two chances are pending. The partner spends its own chance, and the donor is chosen after the partner, in the same order among the remaining holders. A waiting holder that is the arriving visitor's only local partner therefore converses instead of donating, whatever its identity: two stops, two chances. If the only pending chance is the local partner's own, no conversation forms and the partner keeps waiting. A citizen holds at most one chance. When a drawn visit already carries one, the visit uses or keeps one, and the other **passes on** to the first citizen in ordinal identity order that holds neither a chance nor a stop. A repeated visit therefore retains its second chance only when the bound allows it and an available recipient holds neither a chance nor a stop record.

Pending chances are **bounded** by four rounds of draws for the whole city (4 × setting% of residents) and never exceed one per resident, so from 25 up the bound is every resident. This 4× capacity factor was chosen empirically on the commuter fixture, not derived as a guarantee for every routine or geometry; it does not multiply the draw probability. A new draw or passed-on chance that cannot be used or stored is dropped: storage can fail at the cap or because every potential recipient already holds a chance or a stop record. Even an expired stop record occupies its resident's slot until the visit clears it, so recipient capacity can run out below the nominal cap. After the setting is lowered, chances inherited from a save at the higher setting that exceed the new bound are **trimmed** at the first eligible visit, lowest identity first and holders away from leisure venues first, and counted as dropped. Inherited chances within the new bound are kept and spent like any others. In particular, lowering from **26–99 to 25 or above** retains up to one chance per resident without trimming; the inherited stock can keep stops at the feasible share for several game days (about three in the review's 50 → 25 probe). At **100** every visit is drawn, so nothing can be handed on and nothing is banked. An unused chance survives only while its holder waits at a leisure venue for a conversation partner; any other holder loses its chance at the next eligible visit. A later decrease from 100 therefore inherits at most those waiting partners. At **0** nothing is drawn, used, handed on or trimmed. Chances already pending from a save at a higher setting are kept, not spent, until a later non-zero setting uses or trims them, so 100 → 0 → 25 behaves like 100 → 25.

Every draw is therefore realised once, still pending or dropped (drawn = realised + change in pending + dropped; a conversation counts its partner's chance as realised). Repeated visits, stay length and admission timing do not lose chances provided storage remains within the bound and every required pass-on has an available recipient holding neither a chance nor a stop record. The realised share reaches min(setting, feasible share) once the backlog of chances drawn at infeasible visits has been spent, provided neither storage constraint causes drops. It can fall short through pending backlog or permanent loss:

- **Chances still pending** count as drawn but not yet realised. When the setting is close to the feasible share the backlog is large relative to a short run. In the 10-day mixed-routine test, 25 realises 23.4% against a 25.1% feasible share, with 250 chances pending. In the 112-day commuter test the backlog clears: 25.0% against 25.3%, with nothing dropped.
- **Storage exhaustion causes drops**, either at the cap or when no recipient slot is free, even below the cap. A multi-day surplus can exceed one chance per resident, for example weekdays of infeasible commutes before days of mostly feasible visits. A 112-day commuter probe at 50 against a 47.9% feasible share realised 43.7%. The committed all-shops boundary fixture also realises only 46.214% at 50 against 53.739% feasible, with 7,614 drops reconciled and no closing pending balance; its matched default arm reaches 25.009% without drops. These high-setting deficits are accepted limitations, not target shares. Holding more than one chance per resident needs a saved counter and schema 17, outside this PR's schema-16 scope.

A chance can move between activities, places and citizens: an infeasible worker break can become a window-shopping stop at another venue, so realised stops concentrate at venues with validated geometry. Ordinal identity order also favours the lowest-identity residents: pass-ons repeatedly choose the lowest free identity, and donor/partner selection is ordinal too. Totals remain correct and deterministic, but some residents stop more often than others, particularly with narrow geometry. The rig must report stops per citizen, including the lowest-identity group against the rest. A seeded-hash recipient order would change behaviour and only move the favoured group; it is a possible realism follow-up, declined for this PR. After a long period with no feasible stop (for example cold geometry after load), the next feasible visits receive stops until the accumulated chances, at most the bound, are spent. At 25 that is a cold day's draws, roughly a quarter of that day's eligible visits. Drain time depends on the feasible-share margin above the setting, so a backlog can take much longer to clear when that margin is small. Because a pending chance usually exists at 25, stop planning runs for almost every eligible admission there, about as often as at 100.

Core decisions read only saved city state and the event order. With a deterministic environment, the same save and seed produce the same stops, and reload never re-rolls the visit draw. In native play, whether a visit has a feasible stop also depends on geometry sampling that is not saved: the per-frame facade budget and cold or warm entrances. Two replays of the same save can therefore differ in which undrawn visit takes a chance and which donor it takes. The draw is never rerolled; chance transfers, consumption and trims are deterministic for the same state, event order and environment, while stops retain their original deadlines and can expire or cancel. The hand-on order, the conversation partner search and the pass-on recipient use in-memory indexes rebuilt from the saved state on attach. The diagnostics `CitySimulator.OptionalStopChances` (eligible visits, draws, realised stops, hand-ons, pass-ons, dropped chances; not saved) restart at every attach. That includes a load and a route rebuild after the native travel clock rate changes, so an audit across either must snapshot them first.

Worker opportunities start midway through the remaining shift. Other opportunities start at admission. There is no camera input, frame-random roll or recurring chance while standing. New stop creation requires validated geometry at that admission; a cold entrance deferred by the sampling budget can miss that opportunity. Its saved choice remains (an accepted one stays pending for a later feasible visit), but no delayed stop or retry is invented for this visit. This differs from retaining an already saved stop while its geometry is revalidated. If time before closure or the existing commitment is below the kind's minimum, the stop is skipped; otherwise the shared `OptionalStops.Plan` clips the duration to that commitment and cap.

The stop overlays the existing admitted activity: the citizen's place, activity, original event/deadline, identity and assignments remain intact. The existing routine event owns the continuation. An event that retains the same admitted visit cancels the stop and keeps its record as evidence that the opportunity was used; changing visits or departing clears the record. Stops do not extend activity deadlines, create travel or restart on reload. A visible body must physically return to its entrance before its newly committed journey can advance; a blocked return can therefore postpone actual travel without changing the original commitment. Food outings and existing native-food service have priority: meal outings cannot start a stop, food-owned shops are excluded, and an accepted meal outing or existing-stop food decision cancels any stop. Cancellation does not alter funds or sales.

Chatting forms a pair from eligible persistent visitors at the same open nightlife place. Each member spends one chance: the waiting partner its own pending chance, and the arriving visitor its draw, a chance it carried from an earlier visit, or one handed on from another holder. The setting does not guarantee a matching conversation density. Candidates come from the index of holders waiting at a leisure venue, in stable identity order, filtered to this place, and an unsuitable partner does not block a later feasible one. Both spots and a common deadline are chosen before either member is published. Membership, facing and the common group identity persist. Missing/cancelled partners cancel the conversation; no singleton or anonymous filler is created. The first visitor may wait indoors for a later eligible visitor without creating a separate meeting trip. There is no minimum evening crowd. A visitor who already received a stop cannot be recruited again during that visit, even after the stop expires, an intermediate event runs, or the city is reloaded. Cancelling an interrupted conversation cancels both members; neither becomes eligible for another chat in the same visit.

Audience opportunities currently use pedestrians completing an existing walking arrival at a venue entrance within eight metres of a live performer. They do not divert routines toward a show or interrupt an in-progress road/transit leg. This is an entrance-passer implementation, **not general mid-route audience interception**. Each member chooses an individual timed stop and keeps its own end; one member is a valid audience. Performer disappearance, replacement, movement away from the recorded target or any rain cancels it. The audience is not native stationary-zone population.

## Saved state

City schema **15** introduced nullable `CitizenState.OptionalStop`: kind, place, stable spot/target identity, spot/facing/return geometry, start/end, original visit/event and conversation members/cancellation. The existing citizen activity remains the continuation; there is no second general itinerary or copied identity.

Schema **16** additionally persists nullable `CitizenState.OptionalStopChoice` for the current visit, including declines, accepted stops and pending chances (accepted, no stop yet; these are the waiting conversation partners and the chances a later feasible visit can use). A pending chance is kept after its visit ends, so it can also appear on a citizen at home or travelling, and a passed-on chance on a citizen that was never drawn. Option B reuses these values: it needs no new field or schema. Strict schema-13 and schema-14 migration initializes no active stops; schema-15 migration preserves existing stops. All three migrations leave the unknown visit choice null. While it remains null, the citizen cannot be recruited by a later conversation arrival; a pass-on can give it a pending chance without another admission, after which it can be a partner if otherwise eligible. It preserves balances, receipt evidence, assignments and interrupted journeys. Money in schemas 15 and 16 uses the exact integer-unit fields and codec of current schema 14; schema-13 money and decimal `...Cents` fields in development saves of schemas 14-16 migrate exactly or are refused and preserved, under the [native-money rules](decisions/2026-10-28-native-money.md). Loading an old save never infers a stop for a current visit. A later newly admitted visit is evaluated under the configured setting. Schema-16 payloads must contain both fields, even when null; `OptionalStop` is refused before schema 15 and `OptionalStopChoice` is refused before schema 16. Future/invalid payloads are preserved and refused, never overwritten.

Validation checks kind eligibility, bounded duration, finite geometry, original activity continuation, distinct group slots and reciprocal membership for active conversations. Cancelled/expired records do not restart. Route rebuilds retain the environment and opportunity setting; event working copies own their stop/member copies.

## Runtime and physical continuity

`Runtime/OptionalStopEnvironment.cs` supplies validated geometry independently of the camera:

- Door/entrance stops reuse the off-flow facade sampling in `DwellSpots`, with complete NPC paths and neighbouring-door clearance. Optional stops and opening queues share one city-lifetime cache and one cold facade sample per frame. Destroyed native building references invalidate derived geometry and are rediscovered.
- Chat eligibility checks the native established nightclub category; other leisure categories are not silently treated as nightlife.
- Rest spots are sampled at actual edges of an existing accepted native park volume, inside that volume and reachable on the dry NPC navmesh. No decorative object is assumed to be a usable bench. Bench-specific seating/animations are not claimed.
- Performer-ring points require a live retained performer, complete walking access, small sampling displacement and proximity to a navmesh edge. The performer is read through its native stationary-spawner reference; no spawn method is called. Ring geometry is cached per entrance and live performer image; changes to performer/zone identity, location or presence invalidate it before reuse. Source metadata was inspected read-only from the installed Steam DLL.
- Confirmed missing/unavailable geometry fails closed. A saved facade stop waiting for the shared frame sampling budget remains pending validation: its choice and original deadlines are retained, and no body is admitted to it until validation succeeds. Subsequent frames validate the remaining entrances, including future worker breaks. Rain and confirmed native disappearance still cancel pending stops. Scene identities are revalidated on load; saved deadlines are not regenerated.

The physical layer shares the existing pedestrian cap, radius and RLO reservations. An open-door arrival retains its completed binding through the queued admission event, then joins its selected stop with that same body. A bound arrival can keep its body, walk to the recorded spot, stop and face the target, then walk back to the original entrance. A mid-visit admission emerges from that entrance. Walking to a spot consumes the original stop window; failed approaches cancel rather than extending it. Idle standing does not consume the approach timeout. Return-path failures retain/retry the body rather than popping it at the spot. When an ordinary or meal departure commits during a physical return, its journey is held at the origin, including traffic-first routes and saves. After the body reaches the entrance, walking continues on that body or ownership transfers to the ordinary car/transit path. Queues reserve positions already occupied by optional bodies; an existing queue blocks an optional admission to the same point. Idle/facing uses the current pedestrian body; distinct smoking/phone/clapping animation clips have not been validated or added.

Stop selection, handoff and duration never read the camera. Physical approach failures can only be observed for bound visible bodies and can cancel their saved stops; this cancellation is not camera-independent. It never rerolls a choice, replaces identity or extends the stop deadline.

Opening queues and safe crossing waits remain their existing mechanisms. A red-light crossing has no time cap. Optional-stop rain cancellation does not change routine eligibility or general crowd caps.

## Evidence and limits

Core tests cover scheduling, all ranges, insufficient windows, closure/rain/spot cancellation, eligibility, persistent audiences and paired conversations, cancellation/reload, strict schema migration, malformed/future refusal, original continuations, midnight visits, event-budget equivalence and route rebuilds. The source-extracted physical harness covers movement/facing/return, blocked paths, queue sharing and immutable deadlines. Selection tests cover shared budgets and RLO reservations. `tests/OptionalStopShareTests.cs` checks the option-B share over 1,500 deterministic visits where about 33% have a feasible stop, within 3 points of min(setting, feasible share): long, 45-minute and 3-minute stays; dense, sparse (over 6,000 and 15,000 minutes) and separated admission waves; 0, 10, 25, 50, 75 and 100; conversation-only and mixed individual/conversation cities. It checks exact chance conservation, each hand-on moving one chance, reload at 0 with pending chances, and same-seed, sliced-budget and save/reload equivalence. `tests/OptionalStopHandoffTests.cs` pins the hand-on order (away before waiting, ordinal identity), conversations by undrawn arrivals, a taken-over partner that can no longer be recruited and save/load around those decisions. [Red/green evidence](../CHECKS.md).

The checks above are offline; they launched or installed no game. The [29 October native campaign](reviews/rig/2026-10-29-pr3-optionB/README.md) measured realised share, kind/venue mix and cost once at `b646dae`: 22.6% cold over 10 days, 24.4% over 28 untraced days and 24.7% in the warm continuation. It also provides bounded geometry and visible approach/dwell/return evidence; exhaustive native visibility and weather coverage remain unverified. Re-measurement at the current head is pending. The default delivers about 25% long-run, capped by feasibility; audience opportunities are limited to existing walking arrivals; no release or ordinary-save deployment is authorized.
