## What is implemented

- **Walk, wait and transit legs are never leased.** `TryAcquirePhysicalJourney` now succeeds only for a journey on a traffic leg (a kernel vehicle with a link). The walk-to-traffic branch of `ObservePhysicalJourney` is gone; traffic starts from the logical due event. `TrafficCompleted` ends a lease with its traffic leg and queues the logical event, so the walk after parking runs on the logical clock. `TryHoldPhysicalDeparture` is removed. `PhysicalJourneyLease`, `TryAcquirePhysicalJourney`, `ObservePhysicalJourney`, `ReleasePhysicalJourney`, `IsPhysicalJourneyValid`, `TrySamplePhysicalJourney` and the kernel `SetPhysicalControl`/`ReportPhysicalProgress` callers stay for traffic legs until F2d.
- **Walker follow (`ObserveWalker`).** A walker binding holds its `CitizenState`, the committed `Journey` reference and its own route progress. Each frame it samples the logical pose (`SampleJourney`), advances its own progress with `WalkerFollow.Advance` (projection by `PhysicalProgress.TryProject` on its own leg, up to 16 corners, waits out a zero-distance leg with the logical clock, stops at the start of a car, bus or subway leg, capped at the logical progress) and takes the along-route lag (`WalkerFollow.RouteMetres`). Pace: 0 at or below 0.05 m (it stands at the logical pose and is never commanded past it), native pace up to 1.5 m, rising linearly to 1.5× native at 3 m. The native destination is bounded by the logical pose: on the logical pose's leg it is that pose (re-aimed once the pose has moved 0.5 m), on an earlier leg the leg's corner, so the agent stops before the pose whatever the frame durations, even while the logical clock stands still. The commanded speed is also capped at the lag per frame (`WalkerFollow.CommandSpeed`). A changed journey reference or phase ends the follow.
- **Corrections.** Beyond 8 m, or when the logical leg is not a walk:
  - off screen: the body is moved to the logical pose (`Warp`, kind `Move`), or released if the person is no longer walking (kind `LagRelease`, with the mode handoff below);
  - off screen and only just behind a person who has boarded (lag ≤ 8 m, logical leg not a walk): a handoff, not a correction;
  - on screen (Q3): it hurries at 1.5× for up to 10 s; then, at the next corner (its own leg changes, or it is within 0.3 m of the corner), it stops and fades out over 1 s (`SetFade`, renderer base-colour alpha) and is released (kind `Fade`). Until a corner comes it keeps hurrying; there is no mid-leg fade. A body whose materials cannot show the fade (`CanFade`: every material in a transparent queue with a colour property) is not faded at all: it keeps following until it is off screen and is then moved. A faded person reappears at the logical pose only once that pose is out of the camera frustum; the restriction lasts as long as the same journey and does not cost selection attempts. No body is moved or removed in one step while on screen.
- **Mode boundaries.** A walker that reaches the start of a car, bus or subway leg is released as a handoff (no correction), and its retry cooldown is cleared so a body of the new kind is acquired at the logical pose at the next selection. A car whose lease ends at traffic completion while the journey continues on foot is handed over the same way. Never a hold.
- **Arrival tail.** When this journey's arrival has been processed (the person is at the place, or waiting for its opening, at the route's end), the walker keeps walking to the door at catch-up pace. At the door it joins a vendor line, an optional stop or the door queue (the existing `ObserveArrival`), or goes in (released). Off screen the tail is bounded at 10 s on the follow clock and then released (kind `LagRelease`); on screen it is not cut short, so it never disappears mid-path. A journey that commits during the tail is followed from the door if it starts there; one that starts elsewhere ends the tail at once off screen, or at the door on screen. This replaces `TryAwaitArrival`.
- **Departure tail.** When a new journey commits while a body is returning from an optional stop or a vendor stand, `OnOptionalDeparture` binds the follow to it at progress 0 and the body keeps walking back; nothing is held. At the entrance it follows with catch-up.
- **Telemetry (`WalkerMovementTelemetry`, Runtime memory only).** A bounded ring of the last 512 corrections with sequence number, frame, person ID, kind, body pose before and after (after = the logical pose it was moved to, or where a released body reappears) and the on-screen flag; counters for corrections, moves, on-screen moves, fades, lag releases, handoffs and faulted acquisitions; a lag histogram (0.05 m bins to 64 m, exact maximum) sampled once per frame per follower.
- **On screen** (`PhysicalPedestrianBody.OnScreen`): the body's renderer is enabled, `isVisible`, and its bounds intersect `Camera.main`'s frustum (planes computed once per frame). Unknown visibility (no camera or no renderer) counts as on screen.
- **Console.** `pc movement status` prints `followers`, `lag_samples`, `lag_p50_m`, `lag_p95_m`, `lag_max_m`, `corrections`, `moves`, `on_screen_moves`, `fades`, `lag_releases`, `handoffs`, `faulted_acquisitions`, `last_sequence` and the thresholds in force. `pc movement corrections since <seq>` prints `gap`, `oldest_sequence`, `last_sequence`, `count` and one line per entry after `<seq>`; `gap=true` when entries after `<seq>` were evicted. Both are read-only (`Runtime/MovementDiagnostics.cs`, routed from `Runtime/VendorDiagnostics.cs`).
- **Test-profile key `MovementFaultAcquirePercent`** (`PrototypeSettings`, 0–100, default 0): after selection and any traffic lease, this share of body acquisitions is refused (seeded `System.Random`), counted in `unavailable=` and `faulted_acquisitions`.
- **Harness.** `tools/movement-equivalence` models the follower for walkers (`PresentationDriver.ObserveWalker`; the double has no camera, so every correction is an off-screen move) and adds a walking-only fixture option (`MovementOptions.WalkingOnly`) and a `walking` arm family.

### Technical choices

- Hurry, fade-out and arrival-tail times run on a follow clock that adds real seconds only on frames where the city clock advanced, so a paused game freezes them with the bodies.
- The fade scales the alpha of each renderer's `_BaseColor` (or `_Color`), starting from the colour the renderer shows (its property block's, else its material's), through a `MaterialPropertyBlock` cleared when the actor returns to the pool. It is used only when every material is in a transparent queue (above 2500, `WalkerFollow.MaterialCanFade`), where that alpha blends. Opaque materials are not switched to a transparent mode at run time; such a body is kept until it is out of view instead. Rig check 1 records which case native characters are.
- Thresholds (1.5 m, 8 m, 1.5×, 10 s hurry, 1 s fade-out, 10 s arrival tail, 0.05 m stop, 0.5 m re-aim) are constants in `WalkerFollow` and printed by `pc movement status`; tune them from the rig's measurements.
- On an earlier leg the body walks to that leg's corner (one `SetTarget` per leg). On the logical pose's leg it is re-aimed at the pose every 0.5 m, about four path requests per second at native pace; the pace is set each frame. At or ahead of the logical pose it stands.
- A walker spawns at the logical pose (`PositionOf`) with its own progress set to the logical progress.
- Telemetry is static so sequence numbers keep increasing across a physical-population rebuild within a session.
