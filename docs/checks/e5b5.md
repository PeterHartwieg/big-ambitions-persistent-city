## What changed

### Decision 12 and R12: admission without the original-three guard

- `Core/FoodMenuPolicy.cs`: new `FoodMenuPolicy.LiveMenu(menu, policy)` and `FoodLiveMenuRefusal`. It checks width (1 to 8), duplicates and the name rule. With no policy, only the original three pass (`OriginalThreeGuard`). With a policy, each other product must be canonical, in the published loaded-name image (`NotLoaded` otherwise) and classed Food by the policy's resolver (`MealClass` otherwise). That is the same rule as E5b4's `FoodProductPolicy.Executable`, so the shop market and Core decisions agree on every name. It allocates nothing.
- `Runtime/NativeFoodMarket.cs`:
  - `SupportedBusiness` calls `LiveMenu` instead of `FoodProducts.Known`. The published image, its stamp and `CompleteFoodMenu.Check` still decide the rest through `ContractAdmits`.
  - `DynamicAcquisition` is true only while the coordinator is Active and every Decision 12 capability passed at city start. Fallback and Warming pass no policy to `LiveMenu`, so the original-three guard stays. There is no config switch.
  - `MenuStatus` shows `DynamicIntegrationRefusal` only for a non-original product while dynamic acquisition is off, followed by the reason: `capability failed: <first failed capability>`, the fallback reason, or the coordinator mode. While warming it shows a waiting status instead. Other refusals name the cause (more than eight products, not in the loaded catalogue, not classed Food).
  - `ProductPolicySource` is the E5b4 `FoodProductPolicySource` that decisions read. `AdmissionResolver` gives image admission the policy's resolver once the capabilities passed, also while warming, so a dynamic shop already has a passing image when the coordinator turns active.
  - A catalogue rebuild is not a removal. While the reader has no current loaded-name image, `FoodLivePolicy` (Core, `Core/FoodMenuPolicy.cs`) keeps the last policy that had loaded names, so dynamic shops keep ownership, and captures and sales wait at the existing catalogue gate (`ContractCatalogueWaiting`). The next published catalogue decides.
  - `RefuseRemovedProduct`: when an owned or wanted shop's product is missing from a published loaded-name image (mod removed) or stops being Food, the shop gets a recorded contract refusal. Desire is withdrawn at once, hand-back waits for the boundary, and a return goes through Decision 7 recovery with a fresh image and the commit gate. History rows and receipts are not touched.
- `Runtime/FoodContractDiagnostics.cs` (outside the row's file list, three small edits): `ContractCandidate` uses `LiveMenu`, `Admission` passes `AdmissionResolver` to `CompleteFoodMenu.Check` instead of `FoodProductMetadataResolver.Empty`, and `pc food contract status` reports `dynamicAcquisition`, the product policy state and `priceOnlyCheckoutRefusals`. The E5b3 coordinator lives in this file, so the candidate rule and the admission resolver could not change anywhere else.
- `Runtime/PersistentCityMod.cs` (outside the list, one line): `ComposeFoodMarkets` sets `shops.ProductPolicySource = policy`, the same source passed to `FoodMarkets`.

### Q1: width eight

`FoodMenuPolicy.LiveMaximumOffers` is 8 (the hard limit `MaximumOffers`). `FoodChoiceSettings.FoodMenuMaximumOffersPerShop` defaults to 8, and the invalid-value fallback in `Clamp` is 8 too. The market's constructor default follows. A shop with nine or more products fails `LiveMenu` (`Width`) and the E5a builder (`MenuBounds`) and keeps the game's customers. A smaller configured value still keeps wider shops native.

`Core/FoodDecisionTuning.cs` (outside the list, two lines): revision 1 of the held tuning wire omits the menu fields and keeps meaning 4/1/0 (`RevisionOneOffers`). `TryParse` now sets four explicitly for a revision-1 wire, because the settings default is eight. A capture with the new default writes the 241-character revision 2 with eight; a four-offer setting still writes the 193-character revision 1. Every held image keeps its meaning and round-trips (`HeldTuningKeepsRevisionOneAtFourOffersAndWritesEightExplicitly`, `AHeldDecisionSavesAndReloadsExactlyAtFourAndEightOffers`). A held decision under default settings is 48 bytes larger than before. The tuple 4/1/0 is never written as revision 2, which `IsCanonicalWire` refuses.

### Q2: price-only change

`CompletePricesMatch` became `CompletePrices`, which returns `FoodPriceVerdict`. After native setup:

- a missing, duplicated, zero, negative, non-finite or overprecise row, or a different menu, is `Refused` and hands the shop back (unchanged);
- every row valid but a price different from the approved terms is `PriceOnly`: the sale is refused before any receipt or native order, ownership is kept, and the coordinator image records the live prices at the recheck.

All three price-only refusals (the chosen product's price at the live read, the chosen product's price after native setup, another product's price after native setup) return `FoodSale.PriceChanged` and count in `priceOnlyCheckoutRefusals`.

The resident decides again at the counter. `Core/FoodCommerce.cs` (outside the list) handles any `PriceChanged` refusal with `CounterRedecision`. It releases the refused hold and re-reads the shop's live menu. The selected product comes first if it is still stocked and affordable at its live price, including when only another product's price changed. Otherwise the resident takes the stocked, affordable, scoped product they like best: taste band, then lower price, then name, with no random draw. It buys once more, main only. If nothing fits, the hold is restored and the refusal stands. It runs at most once per checkout, so a price that keeps moving ends as an ordinary refusal. No new decision context is drawn.

Product, equipment, staff, bag and hours changes still hand back through the E5b3 stamp and recheck paths.
