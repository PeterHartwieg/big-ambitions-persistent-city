## What is implemented

- **No traffic leg takes physical control.** `TryAcquirePhysicalJourney`, `ObservePhysicalJourney`, `ReleasePhysicalJourney`, `IsPhysicalJourneyValid`, `TrySamplePhysicalJourney` and the `PhysicalJourneyLease` type are deleted, with the simulator's lease registry, rebase and clock check. Core no longer calls `TrafficKernel.SetPhysicalControl(…, true)` or `ReportPhysicalProgress`, so the kernel never waits for a body and no frame-rate observation wakes a physical head. `JourneyProgress` reads the kernel or the logical clock only.
- **The writer never emits `PhysicalControl = true`.** The live capture (`CaptureTraffic`) refuses a vehicle under physical control. The field stays on the wire. The kernel internals (`SetPhysicalControl`, `ReportPhysicalProgress`, the physical branches in `EligibleAt`, `CurbExitAt`, the forecasts and `Snapshot`) stay, and `Restore` still releases a saved physical vehicle to background travel at its saved coordinate, so an older development city with one still loads.
- **Car follow (`ObserveCar`, `CarFollow`).** A car binding holds its `CitizenState`, the committed `Journey` reference and its own route progress. Each frame it samples the logical pose (the kernel's background coordinate), advances its own progress with `CarFollow.Advance` (projection on its own native road leg with car tolerances, up to 16 corners, never onto the kerb connector, parking pause or walk after the road, capped at the kernel coordinate) and takes the along-route lag. The cap (`CarFollow.Cap`) is the kernel's `MaximumNominalMinutes` while the vehicle holds a link, the logical pose after the kernel completed it, and never beyond the car's own parking point: the end of its run of native road legs. Real car routes continue in car mode with a kerb connector and a 2-minute parking pause that no native car drives; a logical pose there is still the car's run and is followed without a correction (`CarFollow.SameCarRun`).
- **Command (`CommandLimit`).** The existing path: `PhysicalTrafficMotion.MaximumAdvance` on the car's own leg up to the cap, so Gley drives at its native pace and is braked short of the kernel's coordinate. A corner Gley has reported is driven through on the next leg's limit instead of stopping for one frame. A fading car stands.
- **Gley evidence and the corner helpers shape the visible turn only.** A road corner is passed in the car's own progress only with Gley's reached evidence, so the visible turn keeps the native waypoint order. `NativeTargetPermission` lets Gley pop a corner only once the cap reaches it (`PhysicalTrafficCornerPermission.CanReachEndpoint`). `PhysicalCornerHandoff.MustHold` holds pose and yaw after a reported corner while the kernel is still short of the marker's projection. The crediting-only members (`TryHold`, the endpoint and normalized-boundary reconcile and clamp functions) and `Core/PhysicalTrafficPrecision.cs` are deleted with their tests, because nothing credits progress any more.
- **Thresholds.** Catch-up beyond 5 m (Gley at native pace, still capped; counted as `car_catch_up_frames`), correction beyond 30 m or once the kernel has completed the traffic leg while the car still drives.
- **Corrections (Q3).**
  - Off screen: a native car cannot be placed, so it is released (kind `LagRelease`, `body=car`, before pose and logical pose) and a body is acquired at the logical pose at the next selection without the retry cooldown, once that pose is out of view. Close behind a person who has just parked it is an ordinary handoff.
  - On screen: the car keeps catching up for up to 10 s; then, at its next corner (own leg changes or within 1.5 m of its corner), it stands and fades out over 1 s (kind `Fade`, `on_screen=true`) and is released. The person reappears at the logical pose only once that pose is out of view. A car whose materials cannot fade (`CanFade`, the walker rule `WalkerFollow.MaterialCanFade`) keeps following until it is off screen and is then released. No car is moved or removed in one step on screen.
  - The journey ends while the car still drives (arrival, or a replaced journey): on screen it drives on to its parking point under the same rule, off screen it is released. A car that fades after its person arrived keeps the restriction at the arrival place, as an arrival-tail walker fade does, so a door queue or optional stop there gets no body in view.
- **Car appearance.** Model and paint derive from the stable car ID (`CarAppearance`; a saved assignment from an older city is kept). Body acquisition no longer writes `CarState.ModelId` or `PaintSeed`, so a seen car saves like an unseen one, also after a failed acquisition.
- **Mode boundaries.** A car reaching its parking point (the road's end) hands over (`car_handoffs`, no correction) and a walker is acquired at the logical pose once the person walks. Off screen, a handoff more than 5 m behind the logical pose lets the walker appear only out of view. A walker reaching its car hands over as in F2c, and a car is acquired once the kernel has admitted the vehicle to a link. Never a hold.
- **Phase mismatch (Decision 6), measured.** For each visible car each frame, `CarFollow.Classify` counts a standing car (under 0.5 m/s) held by the kernel at its red at the link end while Gley's target waypoint is not a stop point (`phase_kernel_held_native_green`), or stopped at a native stop point while the kernel signal of the section the car itself is in is green now (`phase_native_stop_kernel_green`, read through `CitySimulator.TryGetTrafficSignalGreen`; a past kernel crossing is not a green). Episodes and follow-clock seconds are counted. The kernel snapshot exposes `SignalGreen` and `LinkEndNominalMinutes` for this; Gley's light is read from `GetTargetWaypoint(…).stop` (`PhysicalVehicles.NativeRed`). No signal phase is changed.
- **Telemetry.** Car corrections go into the F2c ring (same sequence numbers and gap rule) with `body=car` appended to each `pc movement corrections since <seq>` line (`body=walker` for walkers). `pc movement status` keeps every F2c line and adds `car_followers`, `car_lag_samples`, `car_lag_p50_m`, `car_lag_p95_m`, `car_lag_max_m`, `car_catch_up_frames`, `car_corrections`, `car_fades`, `car_lag_releases`, `car_handoffs`, the four phase lines and `car_thresholds`. `corrections`, `fades`, `lag_releases`, `handoffs` and `on_screen_moves` are totals over walkers and cars. The physical diagnostics line drops `trafficOvershoots` (no lease, no overshoot).
- **Harness.** `tools/movement-equivalence` models the car follower (`PresentationDriver.ObserveCar`, `CarCommand`; the double has no camera, so every correction is an off-screen release) and checks after every frame that no vehicle is under physical control. Its car routes have the Runtime shape (preparation pause, road sections, kerb connector, parking pause, walk). Kernel counters (`TrafficKernel.Reconsiders`, `ProcessedEvents`, `HasPhysicalControl`; diagnostic, never saved) feed the perf numbers. The skipped-event self-check now starves one frame of event units before skipping, because without leases a frame's events always drain.

### Technical choices

- A car is never warped. Off screen the correction is a release plus re-acquisition at the logical pose, restricted to an out-of-view pose like a faded walker. This keeps native Gley ownership rules untouched.
- "Hurry" for a car means no extra speed limit: the command only brakes, so a lagging car drives at Gley's native pace up to the kernel coordinate. The 10 s bound and the corner fade follow the walker rule.
- The fade scales each renderer's base-colour alpha through a `MaterialPropertyBlock` (the walker technique), and restoring puts each renderer's starting colour back into its block, so the paint set at acquisition survives the fade. It runs only when every material passes `WalkerFollow.MaterialCanFade`. Rig check 4 records whether native car materials can fade.
- Thresholds (5 m, 30 m, 10 s, 1 s, 1.5 m corner, 0.5 m/s standing) are constants in `CarFollow`, printed in `car_thresholds`; tune them from the rig's measurements.
- Older development cities are covered by a test fixture (`tests/LegacyTrafficLease.cs`) that rebuilds the state the removed lease left through the kernel internals, so the retiming, reload and release tests for such saves keep their cases.
