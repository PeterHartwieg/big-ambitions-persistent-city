## Population and traffic foundation

The current source writes **schema 14** and migrates valid schema-13 payloads without changing historical amounts or identities; older unsupported development payloads are preserved and refused. The last completed neighborhood food campaign used **0.9.0, schema 12**. The traffic foundation below records earlier milestones. The 0.6.1 traffic implementation passed 832 offline tests and bounded Mac validation. The 0.6.2 change adds explicitly reviewed RLO 0.41.1 compatibility while retaining 0.40.4 and existing citizen saves. It passes 853 offline tests and a bounded [Windows compatibility smoke](docs/reviews/2026-10-20-rlo-0411-pc.md), and is installed and enabled on the isolated PC test rig. The downloadable private package remains disabled by default; full evidence and limits are in [validation status](CHECKS.md). It derives population from eligible residential interiors the player can rent or buy, using RLO's 400 Flare profiles as an immutable donor catalogue. Nearby citizens receive native pedestrian or vehicle bodies; ordinary random street spawns are suppressed after adapter preflight succeeds. Citizens elsewhere continue their saved journeys. The underlying 0.4.0 physical prototype completed isolated Mac validation and four-reviewer approval; the 0.4.1 background-search optimization has separate [review and measurements](docs/reviews/2026-10-19-background-performance.md). The 0.5.0 [daily-rhythm candidate](docs/reviews/2026-10-19-daily-rhythms.md) has offline checks; its matched native comparison remains pending. The [citywide traffic implementation](docs/reviews/2026-10-19-citywide-traffic.md) and subsequent [Mac validation and repairs](docs/reviews/2026-10-20-citywide-traffic-native.md) distinguish offline checks, native findings and remaining gates. Windows performance, extended soaks and user playtesting remain unverified.

- Separate codebase and package. No RLO fork, source changes or upstream approval dependency.
- Required dependency: reviewed RLO **0.41.1** or **0.40.4**. Missing, unready or incompatible RLO leaves the prototype inactive. Going Public sessions are unsupported.
- RLO's **Social Media** option must be enabled and Flare running. If it is disabled after this prototype activates, Persistent City stops while preserving its saved payload; re-enable the option and reload the city to resume.
- Imports the live public Flare population and appearance seeds as an immutable source catalogue. New residents get mod-owned `pc:<sequence>` IDs by remixing that source; RLO retains its original social profiles unchanged.
- Homes respect district preference and capacity. Work/study, shopping and leisure choices sample all eligible places with a distance bias. Native building traffic and explicit attraction weights influence choice. Leisure destinations vary by person and day; committed trips and waits retain their destination.
- Native parks, the outdoor party pier, theatre/cinema and validated Hamptons beach volumes can attract longer outings. Big Beach passed native access checks and received simulated visits; Small Beach is omitted because no complete NPC access was validated. Outdoor attraction weights are policy, not measured visitor counts.
- Residents and their cars remain in the city. Workers can lose or find simulated jobs through bounded daily checks at home; this does not change native employment or RLO's social population.
- Daily work/study, optional shop/leisure visits and returns home; retirees do not receive workplaces. Missing venues remain unassigned. Personal quiet hours vary by resident, persona and day, clipping or skipping optional outings. Work/study and committed journeys/waits take priority; this is a home preference, not a sleep/needs simulation. Most existing outings already end before quiet time; clipping chiefly affects some NightOwl outings.
- Resting residents stay home across midnight until a meaningful activity, lifecycle or preparation event. Commute preparation is staggered 8–14 hours before the next shift and earlier when a previous successful route estimate needs more lead time. Established commuters carry their previous successful route duration into the next shift, avoiding a second route search; unknown, changed or previously unmeasured commutes get an advance estimate. Only the estimate and work window persist; departure chooses a fresh route against current road availability. An unexpectedly long first or changed route can still arrive late, and an unexpectedly shorter departure route can arrive early and wait.
- Actual building entrance samples and complete NPC navigation-mesh paths for walking. No straight-line route across disconnected streets. Physical walkers follow those committed corners with stable seeded appearances.
- Each citizen retains their origin, destination, journey progress and recent admitted visits. An unreachable activity waits/retries; a closed venue does not become a successful visit.
- If a venue opens later within the current activity window, the citizen waits with the destination and deadline preserved. Someone already at the venue records admission when it opens.
- Waiting is indoors (development build, not yet checked in game). A citizen waiting for a route, or waiting somewhere else for a venue to open, is no longer shown standing at a door. Customers who reached a closed shop or leisure venue stand in its queue only for the last 2 to 10 game minutes before it opens, at most 5 in rain, when about half of them stay inside. Staff and students wait inside for their own start. The Core dwell planner (`Core/DwellPlanner.cs`) picks that lead per citizen and day, so a reload or camera move keeps it. Runtime samples up to eight spots along the wall beside the door, 0.6 m out from the facade, on the walking navmesh, reachable from the entrance and clear of neighbouring doors. Places that share one entrance point, such as two customer places in one building, share one line. A door with no usable line is tried again after a minute. A visible walker who arrives inside the queue window keeps the same body and walks on to a spot. A queuer not already shown comes out of the entrance and walks there; with no path to the spot they stay inside. Queuers face the door or the person ahead. Newcomers join at the back, nobody is placed on an occupied spot even if rain starts, people move up into spots freed ahead of them, and a full line leaves the rest inside. Someone already in line when rain starts finishes the lead they started. A queuer who can't reach their spot within 20 seconds of walking goes back inside and waits there for the rest of that wait. At opening they walk in through the door. Walking steps are bounded by time actually spent walking, so a pause doesn't cut them short.
- Waiting to cross is the existing red-light hold. It has no time cap: a walker waits at the kerb until the light lets them cross safely.
- A random ordinary walker that ends its trip in view, within the game's 100 m pedestrian range, pauses for 2 to 6 seconds, then walks to an open business or a home within 60 m that it has a complete path to, inside the game's range, and goes in. With no such door after two pauses (a street of shops at night), it walks out of the picture instead: to a point up to 45 m away that is off screen, or failing that nearest the frame edge, inside the game's range. If it is out of view when it gets there it is returned to the pool; otherwise it pauses and tries again. It keeps its appearance. If the game tries to retarget it on the way (no path, or the door closed), it pauses again and picks another door, so it never heads for the game's old standing spots. A walker held beyond the 100 m range, where the game's own movement code skips arrivals and crossings, stays put until it is off screen. Before, every held walker stood still until you looked away.
- The planner also holds rules for the later dwell kinds in [the proposal](docs/stationary-citizens.md): breaks, chatting outside venues, window shopping, audiences, resting, pickups and buses. Nothing schedules or shows those yet.
- Ordinary-play catch-up handles at most 128 total events, including at most sixteen resident activity events, per game frame. Ordinary advancement stops after a call reaches a soft two-millisecond budget. Native time-machine catch-up uses a separate bounded budget. The first event is always allowed; a native route query can exceed that budget because it cannot be preempted. Physical and traffic snapshots publish once after the batch. The simulation can still lag after a time skip; diagnostics distinguish its clock from the native clock and include publication cost, not total frame or CPU cost.
- Version 0.4.1 reuses road-search memory and avoids repeated native contract checks within each synchronous query. One paired instrumented Mac run measured about 33% less advancement time and 51% less road-search time, with close but different workloads. Peak sampled 6× backlog remained about 90 game minutes; this is not a backlog or FPS fix. All 2,813 controlled same-request native comparisons matched, while the independent full-city runs differed materially. See the [performance evidence and limits](docs/reviews/2026-10-19-background-performance.md).
- The current development source indexes public gates in 50 m cells, reducing repeated all-gate scans while retaining the same intersection rules and conservative fallbacks. Offline comparisons and synthetic timings pass; native frame-time gains remain unverified. See the [gate-access results](docs/reviews/2026-10-21-gate-access-performance.md).
- Car searches in development source stop once they cannot beat an existing feasible route. Access, parking, curb connectors and estimated traffic delay are included; strict ties and faster alternatives retain their choices. Offline comparisons and [synthetic measurements](docs/reviews/2026-10-21-road-search-ceiling.md) are available; native gains remain unverified.
- Per-frame traffic-closure refreshes reuse their relevant-section list and share native validation within one synchronous pass. Every pass rechecks live closures; successful gate checks are retained only for the same immutable gate snapshot. The runtime summary reports closure-refresh cost separately. See the [offline evidence](docs/reviews/2026-10-21-traffic-closure-refresh.md); native gains remain unverified.
- Citywide cars occupy finite directed road sections even outside the view. Sparse events handle entry, FIFO through movement, signals, downstream storage and spillback; partial destination curb exits leave once preceding cars have cleared that point. A resident and their logical car create exactly one demand record. No anonymous demand is added to force congestion. Section capacity (six metres per car), 1.8-second discharge headway and sections up to 80 metres are provisional policies.
- New trips consider current queue delays and blocked streets. These are conditional departure-time estimates, not predictions of every future trip or guarantees of uncongested arrival. Routes already underway stay committed. True closed capacity cycles can gridlock; no cars are teleported or overflowed to clear them. New trips can avoid a proven blocked cycle, but mode choice alone cannot guarantee that existing gridlock clears.
- Reviewed RLO potholes preserve canonical road identities and capacity. New routes include their temporary speed reduction using a uniform average pace across each section; partial curb routes use that same pace. Already committed journeys retain their timing, while visible cars obey native speed limits. Unexplained road changes still stop the adapter safely.
- Physical leases make measured motion authoritative while a body is visible: traffic lights, avoidance and collisions can delay arrival. Releasing a body resumes background travel from its last observed route progress. Rendering never creates a resident or a new destination.
- Schema 14 and valid schema-13 migration are supported. Older unsupported, corrupt, future or foreign-population data is preserved and refused rather than reset. Supported saves retain identities, assignments, logical cars, traffic ownership, committed journeys, consumer balances and receipts. Restored physical leases resume in background until a body is reacquired. A genuine clock-modifier change reevaluates residents safely at home; native-speed conversion preserves current journey positions. Model/paint identity belongs to the saved logical car.
- Pinned Harmony 2.4.2 and its licence are bundled for scoped native hooks. RLO and game assemblies are never bundled. Removing the mod leaves inert own string data in the save.

The game adapter composes complete walking paths with native subway station geometry or directed native road waypoints. Road planning uses this mod's own A* search over the native directed network, not SelfDriving's planner; physical cars use native steering, signals and avoidance. Walking geometry is cached, while road searches recheck live closures and permissions. The main resource saving comes from keeping distant residents without physical bodies; route-search cost has not been benchmarked against SelfDriving. Walking excludes the game's swimming area. Routes compete on estimated door-to-door minutes: complete access/egress walks, preparation, virtual parking, expected subway wait and road congestion. The shortest feasible candidate wins, with stable ordering for exact ties. Saved preference/fare fields remain compatible but do not override faster arrival. The search still bounds station and curb candidates, so this is the best of the evaluated candidates rather than a citywide mathematical optimum. A car remains at its saved logical curb position; residents must walk back to it. Native road legs persist a topology fingerprint and directed node IDs, so the physical adapter can refuse an incompatible route instead of inventing road connections. The first physical version uses **virtual parking**, as agreed: road travel is visible; curb/preparation/parking transitions are background handoffs. Parking spaces and parking manoeuvres are not modeled. Subway riding remains hidden between station walks; no native bus service is exposed by this adapter. A genuine clock-modifier change uses authoritative traffic progress to preserve the measured car position, and preserves exact edge provenance on split native road fragments. Their queue ownership continues in the background; physical driving can resume on subsequent complete tagged edges that pass the existing junction and clearance checks. A partial native edge at the 512-leg route limit refuses timing conversion atomically. Older pre-traffic saves may contain unlabelled fragments; those retain the earlier migration behavior. Unrepresentable unreported endpoint tails during a genuine clock-modifier change are also refused atomically; the mod preserves its payload and stops for that session, requiring the prior clock setting to resume. Ordinary 1×/6× controls do not trigger that retiming.

## Provisional policies and limitations

- Residential capacity uses native `BuildingSizeData.squareMeters` for the rentable/buyable interior, with no whole-property-area fallback. Provisional occupant limits are 0 below 20 m², 1 below 40 m², 2 below 75 m², 4 below 150 m², 5 below 300 m² and 7 at 300 m² or larger. Larger villas therefore remain bounded. These are occupant limits; household relationships and staff roles are unimplemented. Unknown or undersized interiors are omitted.
- A reference inventory of 335 accessible interiors yields 1,064 maximum occupants and a fresh population target of 957. The target is 90% of aggregate occupant capacity, not 90% of housing units. Population scales with the eligible unit inventory captured at initialization. No Hamptons residence passed the reference access/entrance filters; gated private estates remain excluded, and fixed AI villas and special-rival homes also receive pure catalogue eligibility checks. More than 50,000 residents refuses initialization rather than silently truncating the city.
- Sixteen assignments per business/study role remains a provisional allocation. Residents can remain without a work/study assignment when those allocations are exhausted. Native staffing and school capacity need a separate policy review. This assignment policy does not establish native employment or payroll.
- Population creation and strict saved-state decoding run off the main thread after native inputs are captured. The result is published only if the save and RLO donor catalogue still match. Current schema-13 saves preserve existing residents; old development formats are refused unchanged. Earlier migration experiments retained a 25,495-person population, or 1,089 capacity and a 980-resident target with 19 legacy home allocations above the bands. Those archived results describe the retired migration/turnover policy, not current supported loading behavior.
- Existing native ambient vehicles are retired gradually, offscreen and only while native traffic is active and time is running. They can remain during paused initialization or rebinding; the final Mac campaign observed 7–12 such candidates in paused handoffs and none in its sampled unpaused states. This transitional pool is separate from resident-owned logical traffic.
- Native customers/workers, service vehicles, stationary scene actors and RLO scenes remain explicit exceptions. RLO's live social-cast/casualty reservations are observed read-only; an exact matching original Flare resident is withheld while RLO shows them. Generated residents are never matched merely because they share donor appearance data. Full social/employee identity unification remains deferred. The population model does not hire or reassign native employees; resident budget income is an explicit outside flow.
- The general catalogue, assignments and weekly hours are captured at initialization and persisted. Automatically discovered food shops refresh their native opening hours; other live business changes and new buildings do not refresh the general snapshot yet. A map/game update needs a reviewed migration.
- Destination weighting uses capacity, district and straight-line distance, without checking every candidate's route or opening-hour overlap. - View selection and body pools are bounded. A body is disposable; identity, destinations and logical car ownership persist.
